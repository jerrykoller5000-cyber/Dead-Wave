This pass was different from the earlier ones: for the first time the game was
running on YOUR machine — a browser pane in the Claude desktop app, on your
NVIDIA (Blackwell) GPU — with real frame timings and screenshots. Everything below
was measured there, not in the headless harness. That changed the diagnosis
completely, and it also caught a bug the harness could never have seen.


THE FLOATING ORANGE CANOPIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Real, and mine. The instanced-canopy change from cp26 was wrong on a real GPU:
three's WebGPU renderer builds one uniform buffer of instance matrices per
compiled program, and since every canopy's material is structurally identical,
every tree with the same blob count drew with the FIRST such tree's matrices and
colours. Autumn crowns over birches. The harness models the renderer's contracts,
not its bugs.

Backed out for merged geometry instead: each canopy is one vertex-coloured mesh
(the same path the trunks already use, which cannot have that problem), with
each blob's vertex range remembered. Destroying a blob collapses its vertices to
a point; shrinking the survivors scales theirs. One draw per canopy — better than
instancing's two — and progressive leaf destruction works exactly as before.


WHERE THE FRAME ACTUALLY WENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
On your machine the frame was ~13.4ms of pure CPU time inside three's WebGPU
renderer, roughly 5µs per draw call, with the GPU nearly idle. The whole problem
was draw count. Measured in a forest view:

  grass tufts                        1,746 of 2,745 draws (64%)
  tree canopies                        ~210
  trunks                               ~209
  everything else                      ~580

So:

· Ground cover is merged into 30m cells (grass, bushes, ferns, flowers, mushrooms,
  twigs together), ~270 draws instead of ~2,500. The wind sway moved into the
  vertex shader: each vertex carries its tuft's sway amount × its height above the
  tuft's base and its tuft's phase, and a TSL positionNode pushes it along the
  wind. The field moves the way it did. Verified on your GPU by cranking the wind
  to 60 and watching the blades smear sideways.

· Zombie bodies compacted from ~31 meshes to ~15 (16 → 11 shadow casters). Every
  rigid segment between joints is merged into one vertex-coloured mesh; every
  joint still articulates. Dismemberment, hit-flash and corpses all work through
  the merged parts. Verified visually — a lineup of every type looks identical.

· Corpses stop casting shadows once they're flat (a sliver nobody sees, ~200
  shadow draws saved with eighteen corpses down).

  forest view      draws 2,745 → ~1,150     render CPU 13.4ms → ~5.6ms
  20-zombie wave, flamethrower, all weapons: ~11–13ms render, 60fps


THE "RANDOM HICCUPS" — ROOT CAUSE, FROM THREE'S OWN SOURCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Even with the frame under budget, a wave still had four to eight frames over
50ms, up to 180ms. Profiling the renderer's internals on your machine put every
one of them in `Nodes.getForRender`: a full WGSL shader build, ~30ms each.

Reading three@0.175.0's source for the cache key explained why they kept
happening:

1. The material cache key hashes every numeric material property as "zero or
   not zero". So `emissiveIntensity` 1 (fresh body) vs 0.65 (hit-flash) is ONE
   variant, but 0 (flash faded) is ANOTHER; `metalness` 0 vs 0.05 is another;
   `transparent` true/false another. A zombie body goes through three of these
   in its life; a severed limb — which cloned the body's material and set it
   transparent — was a fourth.

2. `Nodes.delete()` decrements a use count on the compiled variant and EVICTS IT
   FROM THE CACHE when the count reaches zero. Disposing a material deletes its
   render objects. Severed limbs, falling leaves, shotgun shells and junked
   corpses were all cloning and disposing materials — so each time the last one
   on the field expired, its shader was thrown away, and the next one rebuilt
   it. Mid-fight. Every time. That is what "hiccups at seemingly random" was.

Fixes, all verified by watching the cache size on your machine:

· Nothing transient is disposed any more. Severed limbs share the body's
  materials (they sink into the ground instead of fading, which needed no
  per-limb opacity). Falling leaves and shotgun shells are pooled. Every zombie
  body is recycled, dismembered or not — stumps are tagged and stripped on reset
  instead of the whole body being thrown away and rebuilt.

· A staged fight runs behind the start menu at load, with the canvas hidden:
  every zombie type spawned, shot (flash on), flash faded, killed every way
  there is (corpse, gibs, severed limbs), plus brass, tracers, fire, acid and
  drops — then a second, live one for ~2 seconds driven by the real loop. It
  has to run from inside the loop: staged from module load it left variants
  uncompiled that the loop's first fight then built, for reasons I could not
  pin down and stopped chasing once running it from a tick fixed it.

  shader variants compiled at menu:     58
  new variants during a 20-zombie wave:  1   (was 8, each ~30ms)
  frames over 50ms in that wave:         0   (was 4–8)


FIDELITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
· Bloom. The scene renders to a texture and a threshold bloom is added: fire,
  muzzle flashes, tracers, lasers, zombie eyes and the kiosk screen are pushed
  above 1.0 so they bleed, and the low-poly mid-tones don't. Night with the
  flamethrower and glowing eyes is the shot to see. (A cleaner selective bloom
  off an emissive render target was tried first; on a real GPU every material
  that doesn't write an emissive output invalidates the multi-target pipeline.)

· Hemispheric shading on canopies: baked into the vertex colours while merging —
  undersides a little darker, tops a little lighter, plus a mild vertical
  gradient. Low-poly foliage reads as solid.

· Contact shading: a soft dark pool baked into the ground under every tree and a
  tighter one under every rock. The sun throws the hard shadow; this is the
  ambient half that makes things look planted rather than placed.

· ACES tone mapping was tried and left off — a modest gain that would have
  re-graded the look you tuned. One line to enable if you want it.


PHYSICS AND ANIMATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The existing animation layer turned out to be thorough already (walk cycles with
knees and elbows, secondary motion, limps, spider gaits, topple deaths with
flailing limbs, hit flinch, attack swings, hurt reactions). Two things were
clearly missing:

· Tree falls. A felled tree rotated at a constant rate and vanished the instant
  it hit 90°. Now it accelerates on t², hits the ground with dust along the
  trunk and a distance-scaled shake, crushes anything in the strip it lands on
  (zombies take heavy damage and are floored; you take 35 and a "TIMBER" banner),
  and lies there ~20s before sinking away. Probe covers all of it.

· Knockdowns. Explosions floor anything short of a colossus; a point-blank
  shotgun blast floors the small types. The body topples about its own feet in
  the direction of the push (hit from the front, falls backward), lies helpless
  ~1–1.5s, then gets up. Brutes only go down to explosives. Cooldown so a
  grenade spam can't keep the horde permanently flat. Probe covers direction,
  resistance, cooldown, dying while down, and clean recycling.

GEAR AND ARMOR
  The marine's helmet, plate carrier (with its pouches, radio and patch), knee and
  elbow pads, and the NVG mount are now registered as gear parts on the model and

  Armor is a pool (helmet 25 / vest 50 / pads 15) shown as a steel-blue ARMOR bar

  buying gear mid-game costs nothing — verified on the GPU: 58 variants before and

SOUNDTRACK — REPLACED
  The old assets/music pack was 20 three-minute loops of the same eight bars each
  (measured: 0.97–0.98 spectral self-similarity between any two 8-second windows,
  near-constant loudness), all bright chiptune, picked at random and cycled
  regardless of what was happening. That is the monotony you heard.
  The new score is generated by tools/compose.py — a small arranger and synth in
  numpy: 15 tracks in seven mood pools (menu, day, night, fight, boss, fallen,
  dawn), each with a chord progression in its own key/mode, a section form that
  adds and drops layers (pads, bass, arps, plucked strings, e-piano, bells, lead,
  drums), a motif-based lead line that snaps to chord tones on strong beats and
  cadences on the root/fifth, section-end fills, dotted-eighth delay and a
  Schroeder reverb, then a soft bus compressor. Measured: 8-second windows of the
  new fight tracks correlate at ~0.5 (variety), loudness swings ×3 between
  breakdown and climax, and the mix is bass-forward but not boomy (A-weighted:
  ~30% sub/bass, ~30% mids, ~20% presence).
  In the game a music director (AudioSys.updateMusic) reads the state every frame
  and crossfades two <audio> decks: menu → day/night prep → fight (instant, with a
  horn stinger; boss variant on blood moons / colossus days) → "Day N cleared"
  fanfare → back to prep. Calm moods hold 6s so dusk can't flap the music; leaving
  the menu or a fight is immediate. No track repeats back to back; calm pools rest
  2.5–6.5s between tracks. Music ducks at low health, in the shop and when paused.

AMBIENCE AND SFX
  A continuous bed (AudioSys.ambienceStart/updateAmbience): two wind layers that
  gust on a slow wander and get leafier in the forest, a river layer by distance to
  the water, two trilled cricket voices after dark with irregular bursts, birds by
  day, owls and distant groans at night, thunder in the rain. Every one-shot can
  now pan and send to a convolution reverb (a synthetic 1.6s outdoor impulse), and
  the guns got low-end tails and reverb sends sized to the weapon: pistol a slap,
  shotgun a wide splash, sniper a rolling echo. Grenades patter debris; felled
  trees creak, crack, thump and shed leaves; wave starts are a long horn over a hit.
