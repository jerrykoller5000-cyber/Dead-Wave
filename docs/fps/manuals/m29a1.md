# M29A1 81-mm mortar (the game's mortar, changed from the 60 mm M224: Jerry, 8 Nov "That's fine: change the mortar") — digest
Source: https://www.liberatedmanuals.com/TM-9-1015-200-10.pdf — TM 9-1015-200-10, Operator's Manual, Mortar 81-mm M29A1
(23 Oct 1985, Changes 1-5 to 1996); read in full as text; the illustrations are images [figure only] (end items App B
pp.B-5 to B-8, basic issue B-9 to B-15). (m224.md stays as the old note.)
The system: cannon M29A1, bipod M23A1, baseplate M3 (one piece), sight unit M53/M53A1 (the M109 elbow telescope on the
M128 mount), aiming posts M1A2 with M14 lights, the M45 boresight, the M53E1 instrument light, covers, fuze wrench and
setter; the M125A1/A2 carrier (an APC turntable) also covered. For the game: the cannon, bipod, baseplate, sight, and the
rounds (posts optional).
Specs (pp.1-9 to 1-11): cannon 28 lb (12.7 kg), bipod 40 lb (18 kg), baseplate M3 25.4 lb (11.6 kg), sight 5.25 lb
(2.4 kg): about 99 lb in all. Overall length 51 in (1295 mm), height on the bipod 37.5 in (953 mm), carrying width 14.3
in, max width 21 in; the baseplate 21 in (533 mm) across. Elevation about 800-1500 mils (about 10 mils a crank turn);
traverse about 95 mils each side of centre (19 handwheel turns), all round by moving the bipod round the baseplate.
Sustained 3 rpm at charge 8 (5 at charge 6); maximum 12 rpm for 2 min (charge 8) / 5 min (charge 6). Max range about
4,737 m (the M374A3/M375A3 4,800 m).
Parts: the CANNON: a smooth-bored muzzle-loaded tube with a thread at the muzzle, a BARREL RING clamped part-way along
it with a lug (it fits the bipod's rod-end clevis) and two white lines either side of it (the ring sits on top, centred
between the lines, in the yoke); the BASE CAP at the breech with a ROUND (spherical) projection that seats in the
baseplate's socket and two flats for alignment, and a fixed FIRING PIN inside (drop fire). (Known, not in the text: the
M29A1's tube has external spiral cooling ribs on its lower half.) The BIPOD: the yoke (swung up horizontal to take the
barrel), the clevis locking pin, the rod-end clevis, a spring SHOCK ABSORBER with a locking sleeve, the ELEVATING
MECHANISM (a housing on the centre column with a projecting lug and the ELEVATING CRANK below), the TRAVERSING mechanism
(a crank for coarse and a handwheel for fine, the traversing nut), the CROSS-LEVELLING mechanism (a sliding bracket with a
locking sleeve, an adjusting nut), two LEGS joined by a doubled CHAIN hooked to the left leg, the feet (set in sandbags on
soft ground), the SIGHT SOCKET (a dovetail, beside the traversing crank). The BASEPLATE M3: round, one-piece aluminium,
21 in across, a U-shaped ROTATING SOCKET (it turns all the way round; the U's open end points the way of fire).
The SIGHT UNIT: the body with the elevation knob and its coarse scale and micrometer, the deflection knob with a red
fixed scale (3200 mils reference), a black slip scale and micrometer, two level vials (cross-level and elevation) under
covers, a locking latch and the dovetail; the M109 ELBOW TELESCOPE with an eyeshield on the M128 mount; a vertical
reticle line.
Emplacing (pp.2-24 to 2-34): the baseplate set on firm ground (a sandbag ring on hard ground) and jumped on to seat it,
the socket turned so the U opens toward the target; the bipod lifted (left hand on the traversing crank, right on the
sight socket) and its legs set about 2 ft in front; the chain unhooked and the legs opened to its length; the elevating
mechanism moved until the traverse is level, the sleeve tightened; the yoke raised level; the muzzle cover off; the
barrel laid into the yoke, ring up between the white lines, its lug into the clevis, the clevis pin in; the base cap's
ball into the U-socket and the cannon given a QUARTER TURN to lock it; the sight put in the socket (800 mils, red scale
3200), laid on the aiming posts (the far one about 100 m out, the near one halfway). Out of action the reverse: the
sight off, the barrel lowered, the quarter turn back, the cannon lifted out of the socket and the yoke, the bipod
traversed and its clevis locked to the elevating lug, the legs closed and the chain wrapped, the baseplate lifted.
Firing (pp.2-64 to 2-65, 4-13): the round unpacked (never dropped), the bags and stop off, the fuze set, the charge
adjusted (increments taken off the tail to the firing table; the rest slid against the fins on the M374A3/M375A3), the
safety pin/pull wire out just before loading; the loader holds the round near its middle, puts it in FIN END FIRST,
lets go and drops his hands down and away from the muzzle, turning aside; it slides down onto the firing pin and fires;
the cannon recoils into the bipod's spring shock absorber. Misfire: shout it, all but the gunner clear, the barrel
kicked, a minute's wait, the sight off, the barrel lowered, the quarter turn, the cannon lifted to level (base never
below the muzzle), the round caught at the muzzle with the thumbs.
Rounds (ch.4): HE M374/M374A3 olive drab with yellow markings (PD fuzes M524/M567/M526, VT M532); WP smoke M375 light
green with a yellow band and light red markings; illumination M301A3 white with black markings (a time fuze M84A1, a
parachute candle); practice M880 blue with white markings and a brown band; training M68 black with white. Propellant
increments on the tail boom: M90/M90A1 nine in bags, M185 eight in bags, M205 four HORSESHOE-shaped containers (on the
A3 rounds), M2A1 four cellophane bundles; an ignition cartridge in the boom. (Known: an 81-mm HE round is about 0.5 m
long and about 4 kg, a teardrop body with a long finned tail boom.)
For the game: the mortar grows from a 60 mm to an 81 mm system (taller cannon on a heavier bipod, the bigger round in his
hand and in flight). The gameplay numbers (range, damage, rate) stay unless Jerry says; the carried and emplaced forms
stay one marine's.

The laser/light (J46, Jerry 8 Nov): "Mortar gets no laser light."
