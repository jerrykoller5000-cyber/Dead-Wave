# The optic upgrades (Jerry, 8 Nov ~04:30Z: "For optic upgrades.") — digests
Today the game has no optic slot in the armory (the mods are the extended mag, heavy barrel, suppressor, the pistol's
auto sear, the saw's tank); the sights are built into each model (the GW-4's holo, the sniper's scope, the rotary gun's
and launcher's reflex sights). These four are the upgrades' models and sight pictures.

The red dot on top of the ACOG (Jerry, J51: "We can only have a red dot on top if you have an efficient way of cycling
between the two."): the switch is the click the sniper's scope already uses: a MIDDLE-CLICK flips between the ACOG (4x)
and the RMR on top (1x), and while aiming one WHEEL notch does too (up = the ACOG, down = the RMR, as the sniper's wheel:
up for more power, no wrap). The view rises to the RMR (or drops to the ACOG) in about 0.15 s without leaving the aim;
it can be picked before raising the gun; each gun remembers its last choice; the zoom label (#scopeZoomLabel) shows 4X
or 1X. The ACOG model carries both sight points (userData: the ACOG's eye line and the RMR's). If the switch doesn't
feel quick on the 5080, the RMR comes off the ACOG.

## Prism 3x (Vortex Spitfire 3x, SPR-1303) — read from manuals.plus (Jerry's third link)
Source: https://manuals.plus/asin/B0FMT1SMDZ.pdf — a text rendering of Vortex's "Spitfire 3x Prism Scope Instruction
Manual" (SPR-1303, the EBR-556B reticle). The article and B&H links could not be read. The figures (1.1, 1.2, 2.1, 3.1)
are images.
Specs: 3x; aluminium, black; nitrogen-filled, O-ring sealed; about 0.35-0.46 kg with the mount (the sources disagree);
(Vortex's catalogue: about 5.5 in long, eye relief about 2.8 in, 31.5 ft field at 100 yd). A MULTI-HEIGHT MOUNT with two
heights over the rail (30 mm and 40 mm, by a riser), clamping to a Picatinny rail (two rail pieces, four screws in the
kit). Illumination red OR green, five levels each, one control; one battery under a screw cap (about 250 h at the
brightest, 3,000 h at the lowest). A fast-focus eyepiece (the reticle focus ring); flip-cap lens covers; windage and
elevation turrets under two caps.
Parts in the manual's figure 2.1: the ocular lens, the reticle focus ring, a flip-cap lens cover, the battery
compartment, the elevation adjustment, the windage adjustment, the objective lens, the Picatinny rail mount. (Known shape:
a squat prism body with the turret block in the middle, the elevation cap on top and windage on the right, the
illumination and battery on the left, a short objective bell and a slightly larger ocular with the focus ring.)
Reticle: the EBR-556B, ETCHED on the prism (it shows unlit): (known) a circle-and-dot centre for the 100/200 zero, a BDC
ladder below for 300-600 yd with small wind dots, illuminated red or green. 3x down the sight.
For the game (J50): the GW-4, the KR-7, the revolver and the shotgun (the table above). (J43 had put it on the
Breacher-12 from his AA-12 photo; J50 gives the Breacher the RMR only.)

## Red dot (Trijicon RMR) — manual read (text; figures are images)
Source: https://www.trijicon.com/uploads/page_content/PML1016-1_Rev%284%29_RMR_Manual_web.pdf (PML1016-1 Rev 4).
Models: Automatic LED, Adjustable LED, Dual Illuminated (tritium + fibre, no battery). 1x. Window 22 x 16 mm; body 46 x
28-30 x 25 mm (L x W x H); 33-34 g; 7075-T6 forged aluminium housing; 1 MOA clicks, 150 MOA travel; CR2032 battery
loaded from the BOTTOM (so the sight comes off its mount to change it); the Adjustable has a + and a - brightness button,
one on EACH SIDE of the body (8 settings, 1-2 night-vision only); the windage (A) and elevation (B) adjusters turned with
a coin (Figure 1 p.19 [figure only] — on the RMR the elevation screw is on top behind the window's hood and windage on
the right side, known); two #6-32 flat-head screws fix it to a mount or a slide cut (15 in-lb); mounts and adapter
plates are separate. Known shape: the RMR's sloped "ears" (a protective hood arching over the window, thicker at the
front), the window tinted slightly blue-green, a red dot (sizes from about 1 to 6.5 MOA); black.
For the game (J50): the GW-4, the KR-7, the pistol (a slide-cut mount), the REVOLVER (a plate on the top strap's rear,
Jerry's photo), the Wasp (on its top-cover rail, Jerry's UZI photo), the shotgun, the Breacher-12, the rotary gun, and
on top of the ACOG (the TA31RMR does exactly that).

## ACOG 4x32 (Trijicon TA31 family, BAC) — manual read (text; figures are images)
Source: https://www.trijicon.com/uploads/page_content/MANUAL-4x32_3.5x35_BAC_.pdf.
Models: TA31 (red donut), TA31A (red triangle), TA31F (red chevron, TA51 mount), TA31F-G (green chevron), TA31H
(horseshoe/dot, M4 BDC), TA31RMR (crosshair with an RMR on top), and the 3.5x35 TA11 family (an amber chevron option).
Body and parts (Figure 1 p.6): objective at the front, eyepiece at the rear; the FIBRE-OPTIC LIGHT COLLECTOR, a tube
along the TOP of the body (it brightens the reticle in daylight; tritium lights it in the dark); the ELEVATION adjuster
on TOP and the WINDAGE adjuster on the RIGHT, each under a finger-tight cap that seats on the housing, the caps marked
with U and R arrows; the TA51 flat-top mount under the body (two screws), with an interface clamp bar and two COLT-style
THUMBSCREWS on the left, interface studs into the rail's slots (eye relief by choosing the slot); a carry-handle mount
alternative. Accessories: flip caps, rubber caps, a rubber eyepiece, a kill-flash honeycomb, a dust cover. 1/2 in per
click at 100 yd (4x32). Known shape (not in the text): a stubby forged-aluminium body about 150 mm long, ~280-400 g with
the mount, the objective end squared-off and finned, the collector a ribbed tube along the top; black or FDE.
Reticle (p.23-35): a BDC from 100 to 800 m. The chevron: zeroed at its tip (100 m), its base = 300 m, horizontal stadia
lines below for 400-800 m, each line as wide as a 19-in man's shoulders at that range; the donut, the triangle and the
horseshoe-dot variants work the same way. Some add a horizontal mil scale (5-mil steps to 10 mils each side). Red
(green, amber options). Bindon Aiming Concept: both eyes open — moving, it reads as a red-dot sight; settled on the
target, the 4x view takes over.
For the game (J49, J50): the GW-4 and the KR-7, an RMR on its top; 4x zoom down the sight.

## Sniper scope (Nightforce NX8) — manual read (text; figures are images)
Source: https://www.nightforceoptics.com/content/files/downloads/0422_NX8_OwnersManual_WEB.pdf (04/22).
Models: 1-8x24 F1, 2.5-20x50 F1, 2.5-20x50 (SFP), 4-32x50 F1, 4-32x50 (SFP); all 30 mm tubes. The sniper's: the
4-32x50 F1 (or the 2.5-20x50 F1): 340 mm long, 811 g; objective bell OD 59 mm; eyepiece OD 43 mm; mounting length 145 mm
(front 50, rear 56 mm of tube outside the saddle); eye relief 88 mm; FOV 8.7 m at 4x to 1.5 m at 32x per 100 m; 0.1 mrad
clicks; parallax 11 yd to infinity.
Parts (the callout figure p.3, A-J): A objective, B eyepiece, C eyepiece lock ring, D POWER THROW LEVER on the zoom ring,
E the power zoom ring, F illumination control and battery, G windage under a cap, H the ZEROSTOP ELEVATION turret
(exposed, a "0" mark on the dial and a zero-reference line on the tube), I the PARALLAX side knob (with the DigIllum
push button at its centre: illumination red or green, held 5 s to switch), J capped elevation (1-8x only). Known
placement: elevation on top, windage on the right, the parallax/illumination knob on the left; a compact saddle (the NX8
is short); a sunshade option on the bell.
Rings: off the adjustments, the bell and the zoom ring; forward pressure on the cross bolts; mounted at the highest
power; eye relief set across shooting positions; reticle plumb to the rifle.
Reticle: the manual names none (Nightforce's options for the NX8 include Mil-XT and MOAR-type: a floating centre
dot/crosshair with mil hashes and a Christmas-tree holdover grid below; illuminated centre). F1 = the reticle grows
with the zoom (first focal plane).
For the game: the sniper's scope (it has its own scope today, 34 mm tube on 29 mm rings) becomes the NX8; the scope's
picture (FOV per power) can drive the sniper's ADS zoom steps (it has 2X/4X/6X today).
