## 1. A kit for aircraft shapes (new: `world/aircraft-kit.js`)

Both planes were boxes and cylinders. The kit draws the shapes aircraft are made of: `loftBody` (a skin through
superellipse cross-sections: fuselages, nacelles, tanks, floats; a surface function for things laid on it),
`airfoilPanel` (NACA sections along a span, tapered, swept, rounded at the tip, cut to a part of the chord for flaps,
ailerons, rudders and elevators), `skinPatch`/`skinBand` (windows, doors, seams, panel lines and stencils that follow the
skin), `strut`/`tubeAB`, `camo` (vertex-colour paint), `mergeStatic` (one mesh per material, vertex colours carried)
and `stencilTexture`. THREE is passed in, so it serves the game and node alike.

## 2. Heron (`world/heron.js`, `buildHeron`; the API, the path and the berth are unchanged)

A twin-turboprop high-wing utility floatplane: a squared lofted fuselage with a sloped two-pane windscreen and
cockpit side windows, five cabin windows a side, a two-leaf cargo door left and one right, crew-door and panel seams,
door handles; a strut-braced wing with camber, two flaps a side (a little down at rest) and ailerons, flap-track
fairings, fuel caps, nav lights and strobes; long nacelles with spinners, chin intakes, oil-cooler scoops, exhaust
stacks both sides with soot behind, three twisted blades with yellow tips; a swept fin with dorsal fillet, rudder and
ventral fin, a tapered tailplane with elevators, beacons, tail light, a wire antenna, whip, blades, loop fairing,
pitot; stepped floats (V hull, upswept bow, raised stern) with non-slip walkways edged in yellow, hatches, cleats,
bumpers and water rudders, on N-struts, jury struts up to the wing struts, spreader bars and crossed bracing wires,
and a boarding ladder each side; camouflage in two greens and a brown over olive, a pale belly; "PGB · HERON",
roundels on the fuselage and wings, "H-07" on the fin, "NO STEP" on the wing roots.

Kept for the game and t158: `group.name 'heron'`, a direct child `heron-wing`, two `heron-float`, two `heron-prop`
(each with `heron-prop-blur`), `heron-strut`, `heron-nacelle`, `heron-landing-light`, `userData.{props, glass,
lightMat}`; floats where they were (x ±2.4, walkway at y 0.42, z -3.7 to 5). **50 meshes before, 32 now.** It takes a
few tens of ms to build, and the craft is created when it is called in (night 20, the last push), so one is built
ahead while the page is idle (`requestIdleCallback`) and createHeron takes it. Checked in the game: t158's checks
by hand pass (the craft, wing and floats, waiting at the dock 1.0 m off its end, props turning, gone after).

## 3. The freighter (new `world/cargo-plane.js`; index.html's `makeCargoPlane` now calls it)

Same aircraft, size and angles: round lofted fuselage with the belly rising to the floor at the ramp's hinge, a
stepped flight deck (six windscreen panes, side and eyebrow windows, chin windows) over a dark radome, crew and
paratroop doors, troop windows, panel lines, gear sponsons faired into the flanks with their door seams; a tapered
wing with dihedral, flaps, ailerons, track fairings and a wing-to-body fairing; four long nacelles with cowl lips,
chin intakes, exhausts and soot, four broad paddle blades with painted tips; drop tanks on pylons; a tall swept fin
with fillet and rudder, a tapered tailplane with elevators; the open bay: lined interior, floor, roller rails with
rollers, folded troop seats, static-line cables, the jump lights, the ramp with ribs, rails, toe and actuators.
Serial "PGB 61-2207" on the fin, roundels on the fuselage and wings.

`userData.props` (4) and `userData.tick(dt, spin)` as before (the beacons, strobes and jump lights blink the same).
**189 meshes before, 30 now.** It is built once (the warm-up's plane, under the menu) and every plane after is a
copy that shares its geometry, with its own lamp materials; `airborne.dispose` now leaves geometry marked
`userData.shared` alone. A supply drop's plane costs ~1 ms to make.

## 4. The parachute straps (`assets/intro/airborne.js`, index.html `beginMarineInsertion`)

Why they missed: the lines ran from the canopy to two points 1.45 m over his feet, ~25 cm over and 20 cm outside the
straps; the straps were boxes on the marine's root, not his torso, and his torso was turned 31 degrees inside them
(the aim twist, frozen for the drop); the hands were out wide, holding nothing.

Now: `airborne.harness(torso)` dresses him in a harness on his torso, fitted by rays at his own torso meshes (not the
arms or head): main lift webs up the chest, over the shoulders to a three-ring connector on each, down the back; a
chest strap and buckle, a waist band, the container strapped on over his ruck with side straps to the waist band;
front and rear risers each side from the connectors up 0.6 m to the links; steering lines from the rear risers' guide
rings to toggles in his hands. `airborne.attach(chute, left, right)` runs the canopy lines to those links every frame.
The torso is squared to the canopy for the drop (it eases back as he lands), his arms are up with the hands on the
toggles. On landing the hands let go, the links come down onto the shoulders as the canopy folds, and the lines lie
from his shoulders to the canopy on the ground until it fades. Crate chutes keep the old rig.

Found on the way: a mesh in the game that does not receive shadows can have its far faces drawn in the sky's colour
(the container looked cut in half from behind; fine in WebGL). Every harness piece, and every part of both planes,
now receives shadows. Worth knowing for anything else built with `receiveShadow` off.
