## 1. The trees: who reads them, who depends on them

The forest today: `scatterTrees` (index.html:17959) places a **target of 430** trees from seed `mulberry32(0x7e44a1)`, out to `LAND_MAX + 26` (past the play boundary, up the foothills). Placement rules, in order: 15 m clear of spawn; no water (depth > 0.02), slope ≤ 0.5, height ≤ 46 m with thinning 30–46 m; river kept open (`distToRiver < RIVER_WIDTH + 1.6` out, banks sparse to +4.5); `nearPOI(x, z, 10)` and `distToPath < 2.2` out; density by moisture (`rnd() > 0.22 + moisture` out — this is what makes glades and thickets today); scale 0.55–1.85 (4.5% giants at 1.85–2.2); trunk out of the wall band; spacing `4.0 + scale × 1.35` against already-placed. `makeTree` (index.html:17828) builds trunk/crown/canopy meshes plus `leafPieces` (index.html:17855) — the per-clump canopy units everything visual reads through `leafPiece*` (index.html:17754ff). W2's "many times today's trees" multiplies every reader below; the ones with per-frame or per-tree costs are flagged ⚠.

**Rendering / batches.** Tree batches (MU-13 §2.4): `buildTreeBatches`/`updateTreeBatches` (index.html:18193/18264) ⚠ per-frame snapshot diff over a third of members per frame; `t50` asserts most trees batched and tree draws < half the tree count (tools/tests/t50.js:19, 25) — a 5–10× forest keeps the *draw* shape (2/cell) but multiplies snapshot cost, cell vertex counts, and the live-tree count near the player (live = inside ~46 m, where density is what CL-287 raises). The canopy fade (`updateTreeFade`, index.html:54227ff) ⚠ loops all trees per frame to fade canopies near the player/camera line. Leaf pieces: damage pops (index.html:52191ff), burning per piece (index.html:52388ff) ⚠.

**Physics / collision.** Trees are *not* in `worldSolids` as trunks; bodies resolve against them via `resolveTreeCollisions` (exposed index.html:61182 region) and the flow field marks each standing tree's trunk (index.html:54055ff region: stump 1.15×, alive trunk radius, falling = none). Felled trees push `kind:'log'` solids into `worldSolids` and fire `dw-log`, which force-rebuilds the flow field (index.html:54162ff listener). `addLogSolids/removeLogSolids`, `TREE_LOG_LIE` (TT exports). A thicker forest = more `ffMarkCircle` calls per field rebuild (every tree in the 160 m grid, every 0.4 s) ⚠.

**Damage / felling / fire.** `damageTree` (index.html:52191: shot leaf pieces hide by health share), `igniteTree` (index.html:52292), `updateBurningTrees` (index.html:52368), `beginTreeFall` (index.html:52608), `updateFallingTrees` (index.html:52780), `restoreTree`. **Missions / world ties.** The hikers' kit tree pick scans standing trees round the hikers' camp (MU-12 §2.4; `pickHikersTree`, game/hikers-kit.js) ⚠ — in thick woods the "nearest bank tree" gets nearer and the rope/bough geometry (reach past canopy) matters more. ## 2. How the dead move today

There is **already a flow field** — CL-287's navigation layer is an extension, not a greenfield. The stack, bottom to top:

- **The field** (`ffPlayer`, plus `ffHouse` for Defend): 128×128 cells at 1.25 m = a **160 m square centred on the (lead) player** (index.html:53952), rebuilt every 0.4 s (`FF_PERIOD`, index.html:53955) or on force (destination change, `dw-log`). Build (`updateFlowFieldOn`, index.html:54007): heights sampled per cell (with landmark decks read as walkways), wet cells cost 2.5×, blocked cells from **trees, rocks, worldSolids, the HQ footprint, and player builds** (each via `ffMarkCircle` with `FF_CLEAR` 0.45 inflation, or exact `pushOutOfBuild` tests), climb limit `FF_CLIMB` 0.47/m, Dijkstra out from the destination (other living players are extra seeds, GB-100). Heights slide instead of resampling when the grid moves (CL-140). `flowDir(x, z)` (index.html:54227) walks downhill, with a nearest-open-cell escape when shoved into a margin. Cost: one rebuild is a 16,384-cell Dijkstra — `ffPlayer.lastMs` is exposed for the bench (TT `flowField()`, index.html:61173).
- **Who follows it:** in `updateZombies` (index.html:56364), a chasing zombie with target within 4.5 m of the player follows `flowDir` (eased over ~0.1 s, index.html:56626), or `houseFlowDir` when marching on the cabin in Defend; `hordeOpenWay` (index.html:56263) also consults it to veto a slot line that crosses an obstacle. Guards asleep at posts, wanderers on trails, lured/window/below zombies do **not** read it.
- **The horde layer** (game/horde.js, GB-94): each zombie gets pace/wit once (`hordeJoin`), then slot steering — `slotAngle/pickSlot/roleSlot` (game/horde.js:131–242), approach points with target leading (`approachPoint`, `leadOffset`), muster/rush bands (`hordeFrame`). In the loop: a chase takes its slot's approach point as the *heading* only (index.html:56520ff region), target/dist stay on the player, so leapers/spitters keep their own ranges.
- **Local steering** (the fallback everywhere the field doesn't speak): direct seek at the target (player, smash wall, decoy lure within 40 m, window route `seekZombieWindow` index.html:36723, defence targets GB-103); the unstick/bluff sidestep — probes 1.8 m ahead, if climb > 0.85 and not a ramp, steers 0.45-forward/0.9-side toward the lower side (index.html:56610ff); separation/spacing from neighbours (`hzSpX/hzSpZ`, HORDE.SPACE_W), recoil ducks/staggers (game/horde.js reactions); then `resolveStaticProps` (index.html:53749) pushes the body out of props/trees/rocks, and builds bash or block per `BUILD_BLOCKS`.
- **Special movers:** POI guards hold posts asleep until woken (near/hit/alarm, index.html:56430ff; `spawnPoiGuards`, posts from bounties/camps), wanderers follow PATHS polylines (GB-89 colossus; `wandererTarget`), cave/guardian/below movement is separate (`updateBelow`), drowned swim, spiders/leapers have their own motion modules. Spawning places via `resolveStaticProps` so nothing rises inside a trunk/rock (index.html:42372ff).

**Where a thicker-navigation layer plugs in (for CL-287 W2 + CL-288 step 4):**
1. **Blocked-cell sources are the plug.** Thicket walls, deadfall and closed paths become `blocked` by adding their solids to the same three inputs the field already reads (`worldSolids`, trees, builds) — no steering change needed; chainsaw-felled logs already demonstrate the full loop (solid + `dw-log` + forced rebuild). "Paths open and closed" = toggling those solids; the field's 0.4 s rebuild is the propagation delay to design around (or fire the existing force-rebuild).
2. **Scale is the real gap.** The field is one 160 m window on the player. W1/W2 woods span the valley (~480 m across with clearings at 110–160 m): dead spawned at a camp 140 m away walk most of the way *outside* the field on direct seek + sidestep. Options the code supports: enlarge `FF_N` (Dijkstra cost grows as cells² — the 0.4 s rebuild is already 10–17 ms when it resamples, CL-140 comment), recentre a second coarse field on the HQ (`ffHouse` is the precedent), or a static valley-wide coarse grid (4–5 m cells) for long approach + the existing fine field for the last 160 m. The worker move (CL-288 step 4) fits `updateFlowFieldOn` as-is: pure typed-array in/out, seeds + blocked sources in, `dist` out.
3. **Trails as costs, not just paint.** PATHS are visual wear + wanderer routes today; the field has one cost knob (wet 2.5×). Making trail cells cheaper (0.7×) is a one-line-shaped change inside the Dijkstra (index.html:54143 region) and gives "the dead follow trails and gaps" (D-103) directly — gaps in thicket read as low-cost corridors if thicket is blocked and trail is cheap.
4. **Not the plug:** per-zombie steering (slots, sidestep, separation) should stay — it's what makes the last 20 m read alive; the field only owns "which way round the woods".

## 3. Building: allowed where, and where "clear ground only" goes

Placement is refused by `placeRefusal(x, z, gx, gz, yaw, opts)` (index.html:34157), called from `tryPlace` (index.html:34409) and the ghost preview. Today's valley rules, in check order:

- Training Ground has its own room rules (delegates to `tg.build.refusal`); below (Hollows) refuses all; off the land (`hypot > landRadius − 1.6`) refuses; **near a cave refuses everything** (`nearCave(x, z, CAVE_BUILD_CLEAR)` — "the ground there belongs to whatever lives inside").
- The shovel: only water/stream refuse it (it reshapes rather than occupies).
- In-grid pieces: `resolveTarget` slot/level refusals; never into the cabin (`houseBoxDist < 0.05` over the footprint points, any height); never onto the player; above-ground slots (level > 0, modifiers, caps) skip ground checks entirely.
- Ground pieces: stream distance, water depth > 0.05, **roughness: `groundSpread(x, z, footprint) > 0.6` → "ground too rough"**, and `overlapsBlocked` (props/landmarks/trees/rocks/builds in the footprint) → the refusal text.

**Where "clear ground only" (D-103: building on some ground) goes:** it is a ground-piece rule, so it lands beside the roughness check at the bottom of `placeRefusal` (the `groundSpread` line) — *after* the level/slot early-return, so decks on walls keep today's freedom, and beside the shovel's exemption question (can he shovel a clearing into buildable ground? W2's chainsaw shortcuts suggest yes for thicket: felling opens ground, which the same check then reads as clear). What "clear" should read: the same data the woods are made of — tree/thicket density or a clearing flag per cell — not a fresh overlap scan (`overlapsBlocked` already refuses a footprint *on* a trunk; the new rule refuses a footprint in *uncleared woods* even between trunks). The ghost preview calls the same `placeRefusal`, so the player sees the refusal before Cash; ChatGPT owns the refusal string key. Design flag for Claude: clearings at sites (W1) must be buildable-or-not by explicit choice — the sites' own yards (trapper's fence, HQ yard) are the existing "cleared" precedents, and CU-196's sightline tool + W1's clearing list are where a clearing registry would live.
