# CL-138: Training Ground performance, ten screamers at 60 fps (Claude, 2026-10-09)

Jerry: "when we call ten screamers out it is pretty much a lag fest unless the graphics are super low. I'm not sure if
ten screamers at 60 fps is an achievable benchmark to hit but please attempt it."

**Result: hit.** Ten screamers screaming, at High, at Max, and at Max with twice the pixels, hold 60 fps on the 5080
(1600x900 camera window). 40 zombies hold 60 too.

## Measured (5080, through the camera's job inbox; frames per second / main-thread ms per frame / draw calls)

| | before | after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| empty room | 60 / render 15 ms / 293 draws | 60 / ~6 ms / 133 |
| 10 screamers | 42 fps / render 20 ms | 60 / ~9 ms / 193 |
| 10 screamers after a few screams | 10 to 23 fps (they called 38 more in, 48 alive) | 60 / ~10 ms (20 alive, the cap) |
| 40 zombies | 23 fps / render 38 ms / 652 draws | 60 / ~12 ms / 253 |
| 40 zombies, Max preset, pixel ratio 2 | not run | 60 / ~12 ms |

The cost was CPU (draw calls), not the GPU: half resolution changed nothing.

## What changed (index.html)

1. **The valley leaves while he is in the Training Ground** (`trainingHideWorld`, called in enterTraining before the
   warm compile, and first in leaveTraining). The room is underground; the whole valley was still being culled,
   matrix-updated and shadow-cast every frame. Every top-level scene object more than 150 m from the room is hidden and
   its `updateMatrixWorld` stubbed (r175 still walks children with matrixWorldAutoUpdate off). It comes back exactly as
   it was, unless something else moved or hid it meanwhile. Lights, pooled effects, zombies and corpses are left alone.
   `TT.trainingHideWorld`, `TT.getHidWorld`.
2. **One draw per zombie body** (`skinZombieMesh`, after `compactZombieMesh` in makeZombieMesh). A body was ~14 meshes,
   ~14 draws plus as many shadow draws. Now the body parts that share textures are merged into one `SkinnedMesh` whose
   bones are the joint groups that already animate (each vertex weight 1 on its part's joint), so every pose, hit
   reaction and crumple from CL-136/137 drives it unchanged. Part colours go into vertex colours; the concept bodies'
   detail textures (GP-161) are kept. Eyes, glows, spit and anything transparent or emissive stay separate (~3 draws a
   zombie now). The original meshes stay, hidden (`userData.skinSrc`), because a severed limb is still flung from them.
   On a dismember the cut joint is collapsed in the skin (`collapseZombieBone`, scale 1e-4); `resetZombieMesh` puts it
   back for the pool. `?skin=0` turns it off for an A/B.
3. **The scream call in the Training Ground** (`trainingScreamCall`): a screamer's call brings 3 through the room's
   gate, counted as the room's own, and stops at 20 alive (`TRAINING_CALL_CAP`). Before, every scream pulled valley
   spawns into the room with no cap, which is where the 10 to 23 fps came from. Jerry: if you want more, raise the cap.
4. **A crash that froze the game on its last picture** (`SCENE_BG`, renderFrame). three's shadow pass sets
   `scene.background = null` while it draws and restores it after. If that draw throws once (a pipeline still
   building), the background stays null, and `updateDayNight` then threw every frame: nothing drawn again, for good.
   CL-117 hit it before. It happened twice today on the camera, once while loading. `renderFrame` now puts the
   background back whatever happened, and the sky code checks.

  colossus): every skinned vertex against where its original part is, walking, after an arm comes off, and after a
  body is reused from the pool. Error 0 mm. The hit flash lights the skin. No page errors.
