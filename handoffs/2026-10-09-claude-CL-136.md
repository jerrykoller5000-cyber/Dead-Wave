# CL-136: the zombie pass, seven points from Jerry (Claude, 2026-10-09)

Jerry: "1. Massive freeze happens when spawning zombies in training and shooting them. 2. Zombies dismembered body
parts have weird artifacts attached to them. 3. Zombies clip into ground upon death in training area. 4. Zombies clip
into walls in training area. 5. Zombies animations suck and all need overhaul. 6. Zombies need unique sounds.
7. Spitter does not actually spit like you would expect." He chose to have me work through them myself.

**Look first:** `review/zombies-cl136/sheet.jpg` (the spitter's wind-up and its glob in the air, a corpse against the
room's wall, a severed arm and head). The freeze numbers are below.

## 1. The freeze: found and fixed

Measured on the 5080 through the camera's job inbox (`tools/probe-dismember.json`, dropped in `review/camera/jobs/`):
spawn ten shamblers in the Training Ground, fire the M4 at them for five seconds, watch the main thread.

| | before | after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| stall per dismembered limb (arm, leg, head) | 405 to 523 ms | 0 ms |
| JS heap per limb | +110 to +170 MB | +0 |
| a kill with no limb lost | 1 ms | 1 ms |
| shader programs or pipelines built mid-fight | 0 | 0 |

It was never the zombies, the spawn or the shaders. `spawnLimbPiece` flung a severed limb by cloning its group, and
three's `Object3D.copy` deep-copies `userData` through `JSON.stringify`. A limb group's userData holds its `elbowG`
and `upper` (objects), so each clone serialised an object tree, geometry, materials and textures included: one
stall and 150 MB of string per limb, which is most of what fighting ten shamblers with an M4 is. The same clone was
behind the carried-gun copies, the offhand guns, the HQ hatch copies in the Training Ground (part of the 0.8 s on
entering) and the camera's first picture (17 s of warm-up, now under 2).

**`cloneLean(obj)`** (index.html, next to spawnLimbPiece; on `TT`): copies the tree, transforms, geometry and material
references, and only the plain values of userData, never another object. Used by spawnLimbPiece, the offhand guns,
carryGunCopy, the skull drops, trainingCopies and mirrorTrainingRack; `tools/camera-rig.js` uses `T.cloneLean`.

A second thing the probe turned up: a limb off a body in the far LOD (40 m+, joints' matrices frozen) kept the frozen
matrices in its clone, so the flung piece hung in the air where it came off. Every piece's tree animates now.

## 2. The artifacts on severed parts

- The stump cap on the flung limb was sized from the limb's WORLD bounding box, so an arm caught mid-swing got a cap
  as wide as its swing: a square slab on its end. It is sized from the limb's own frame now (its cross-section).
- The frozen-in-the-air limbs above.
- The killing hit's red flash never faded on a corpse (the body left `updateZombies` with the flash on): a bright red
  corpse. It fades out in `updateCorpses` now (`c.flashT`).

## 3. Clipping into the ground on death

The topple turns the body about its feet, so a lying body's near half (legs, the back of the torso) went below the
floor by its own thickness. Grass hid it; the room's tiles don't. The body rises by that thickness (`c.lift`,
0.11 × the kind's scale) as it goes over. And in the Training Ground a corpse no longer sinks through the tiles at
the end of its 8 s: the pool dries and it goes in a puff of dust (`spawnDust`). Elsewhere it sinks as before.
Jerry: if you would rather they stayed in the room, say so; it is one line.

## 4. Clipping into the walls

- `trainingKeepZombie` kept every body's centre inside one rectangle, whatever its size, so a brute's shoulders and
  the colossus's chest went through the plaster. Each body now keeps its own radius (+0.18) off the wall faces
  (`tg.zombieRoom`, new in `world/training.js`), inside the stations' margins the rectangle already kept.
- A corpse falls inside the room: `trainingCorpseFit` turns the fall (the reverse, else along the wall) so the head
  end stays in, and a thrown corpse's slide stops at the wall (`corpseMove`).

## 5. Animations: part 1 only

The stride now keeps time with the ground covered: the rate was fixed per kind whatever the body's actual speed, so
a creeping spitter (a quarter pace), a walker in wire, a buffed runner all strode at the same rate, feet sliding over
the ground or running on the spot (`spdK` in the walk cycle; shorter steps at a creep too). That is the one change I
am sure of without Jerry's eye on it. The overhaul itself is not a one-session job and needs him to say what "sucks":
the walk, the attack, the death, the idle, or all of it, and whether he wants it from the studio (baked clips, as the
guardian has) or kept procedural. Proposed: a motion-lab session with him, one kind at a time, shambler first.

## 6. Unique sounds

The kinds had their own voices since CL-130, but every one of them took hits and died with the same two sounds, and
that is most of what a fight is. Each throat now has a death cry (its attack voice falling away, choked, longer) and
a hurt grunt (short, pitched up, 30% of hits), both made from its own attack spec at load (`DEAD_VOICES[kind].death`
and `.hurt`, `core/audio.js`). The demon, colossus, guardian, spider and caveguard, which have no throat, get a growl
falling and a bark (`GROWL_DEATH`). `zombieDeath` and `zombieHit` take the kind now; index.html passes `z.typeKey`.
The fall thud under the cry stays shared. Not listened to on a real speaker yet: Jerry's call whether they are
distinct enough.

## 7. The Spitter spits

It had been changed to "creep" (acid was given to the spider) and so never spat. It creeps as before, and on the
way in it spits: a wind-up you can see (head back, jaw dropping, the gut and jaw glowing brighter, 0.6 s, standing
still, its attack voice), then a glob from its mouth (not its chest) with a spray and a dripping trail, the acid
puddle where it lands. Only with a clear line to him, 4.5 to 16 m, every 3.6 to 5.2 s. The pose owns the head while
it winds up (as the screamer's scream does); `resetZombieMesh` closes the jaw and dims the glow for the pool.
