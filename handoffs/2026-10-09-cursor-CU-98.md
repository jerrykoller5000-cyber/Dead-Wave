## What was wrong

`leg()` built the whole boot inside the `ankle` group. The pose keeps the sole on the ground by turning the ankle
against the shin: `ankle.rotation.x = pitch - hip - knee`, about 40 to 58 degrees in a crouch and 5 to 15 standing in the
ready stance. The turn steps (studio/marine-turn.js) also give the ankle the foot's full yaw. So the shaft, 18 cm tall
round the shin, turned with the foot: upright in a crouch while the shin leaned forward out of it (the black blocks in
review/boots/before/crouch-*.png), and standing, the shin's front came out between the laces.

## The fix

`shinShaft(holder, shin, ankle, lo, hi)` in studio/marine-body.js. The shaft and what is sewn to it (the shaft itself,
the tongue, the laces and eyelets, the side panels and their seams, the heel tab and its pull strap) are built exactly
as before, but into a holder group placed where the ankle is at rest. They are then baked into one mesh per material
in the shin's space (`boot-shaft`, a group under the knee). Each vertex has a weight from its height above the ankle
bone: 1 from 5 cm up (worn on the shin), 0 from 4 cm down (on the foot), a smoothstep between. Before each draw and each
shadow draw (`onBeforeRender` / `onBeforeShadow`), the lower part follows the ankle's own turn, as two-bone skinning
would. The bending triangles come first in each buffer, so only that part is uploaded. Nothing is recomputed while the
ankle doesn't move (changes under 1e-4 are skipped). The foot keeps the upper, both soles, the heel, the lugs and the
welt. Materials, the wardrobe (boot colour) and the floor contact are unchanged.

Cost, measured on the real page: 0.084 ms per bend per boot (2268 of its 2748 vertices bend), only on frames where the
ankle moves. 4 more draw calls per marine (the shaft's leather, laces, stitching and strap are their own meshes now).
No new shaders.
