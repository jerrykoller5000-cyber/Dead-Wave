## Why the rounds missed

The camera looks down on him at about 46 degrees. A round went from the muzzle (about 1.4 m up) to the 3D point under the
cursor. With the cursor on the ground just in front of a zombie's feet, that point is on the ground short of it: the round
dipped into the dirt there (t276 shows it). With the cursor just past its head, the point is on the ground behind it and the
round passed under its chest. Now an aim on open ground is that spot raised to chest height (AIM_CHEST 1.15 m), so the
round flies level that way and meets what stands on the line, as in a top-down shooter. A body under the cursor is aimed at
as before (the pick, GB-206's pull, the lead), and just off one the assist aims at its middle (0.35-0.8 of its height, on
its axis, at the cursor's height within that band: no free headshots), if the line from the muzzle is clear and it is in
range. Not for the launcher's lob, the flamer, blades or the build tools.

## The zoom

The view's tan halves (it magnifies; the camera stays where the orbit puts it). The point under the cursor when right-click
went down is kept under it by moving the camera and its look point together on the ground (the ray through that spot of
the screen meets the point's height at Q; the camera moves by point - Q), so the heading and the angle never change. The
follow-cursor pan is held still while zoomed; the "keep his feet in frame" clamp is off while zoomed (the view is centred on
the cursor now, not on him). The sniper keeps its third-person scope on right-click (its first-person sights are CL-249's).
