## 1. How the height is made (the chain)

One analytic function, baked once into a grid, sampled everywhere after. In order:

1. **`terrainBase(x, z)`** (index.html:3049): domain-warped fbm (warp 34 m at 0.0042 scale), broad rolling landform (±12.5), **ridge lines** `ridged² × 15` faded in from 40→140 m from centre (`ridgeAmt`, index.html:3063 — spawn stays open by construction, ridges grow outward), medium/fine detail, a lake basin (−8.5), a settled core (inside 14–62 m the height is damped to 40% + 0.5), the **mountain wall band** past ~220–232 m (peaks to +135, the map edge), and the lake bowl (floor at LAKE.level − 3.8). This is where W1's "ridges and hollows to break the lines from the HQ" would be added — a *directional* ridge/hollow layer (none exists: today's ridges are omnidirectional noise, faded by radius only).
2. **`solveRiverBed`** (IIFE, index.html:3138): samples `terrainBase` at the river control points, forces the bed monotonically downhill (≤ 0.012/m drop), pins the mouth under the lake, finds `RIVER_JOIN_S` (where banks first stand in the basin). Runs **before** the bake; order matters.
3. **`carveRiver(x, z, h)`** (index.html:3111): channel (2× RIVER_WIDTH) + valley shoulder (5.4×) blended toward the solved bed; dig-only past the join. **`terrainRaw = carveRiver ∘ terrainBase`** (index.html:3129).
4. **`buildHeightField()`** (index.html:3198): bakes `terrainRaw` into `heightField` — 505×505 cells (`HF_N`, 1 m spacing, ±252 m, index.html:3193), ~20 noise evaluations per cell, once at load.
5. **Post-bake edits, in load order:** `flattenBuildApron` (index.html:3219) flattens the spawn clearing to the centre's height out to 20 m + 28 m feather (river-aware fade so it never dams the channel); then world building edits further: cave hills (`caveHillHeight/caveHillAt`, index.html:7301/7312 — domes added over the warrens, read by `terrainColorAt` at index.html:4355 and by the cave builds), `gradeApproach` (index.html:4074 — bridge/dock approach grading), site levelling and the shovel via `reshapeGround` (index.html:4736 — writes **both** heightField and the visible mesh vertices, river-keep aware), and the trapper excavation (heightField depression + mesh cap, MU-12/MU-13).
6. **`sampleHeight(x, z)`** (index.html:3260): the only reader anyone is supposed to use — Training Ground floor and Hollow floor override first, then bilinear over `heightField`, falling back to `terrainRaw` off-grid. Physics, placement, AI, cameras — all of it goes through here (or through `entityGroundY/playerGroundY`, which add decks/platforms on top).

The wall band deserves its own line for W1: the playable disc's edge is `wallStart ≈ 220–232 m` with peaks — the treeline and the mountain wall are the same system (scatter thins by height, MU-14), so "the skyline's pulls" read from `terrainBase`'s wall, not from trees.

## 2. Everything that reads or bakes it

Grouped by *when* they take their height (this grouping is the §3 answer):

**Live readers (sample every frame or per event — they follow any height change by themselves):** player/zombie ground (`playerGroundY`, `entityGroundY`), `waterAt/waterDepthAt` (lake extent mask solved once from the basin, but depth is per-point from height), the flow field's cell heights (resampled on grid slide, MU-14), `terrainSlope`, spawn/placement spot checks (`findSpot`, `historyFreeSpot`, `relaySpawnSpot` — build-time but by sampling), projectile/impact ground, camera ground clamp, the trapper's stepping (`sampleNaturalHeight` snapshot excepted, below), sounds' distance ground. Anything whose position is *computed from* `sampleHeight` at the moment of use.

**Bakes once at load from the height function:**
- `heightField` itself (index.html:3198) + the apron/cave/approach/shovel edits queued after it — a new `terrainBase` means re-running the bake *and every post-bake edit in load order* (they are IIFEs and build steps, not a registry; miss one and that feature floats or buries).
- The terrain mesh: polar geometry vertices take height + `terrainColorAt` (colour/gworn/gwear include cave aprons, path wear, cave hills — index.html:4355), then the wedge split shares those attributes (MU-13 §2.1). Rebuilt only by re-running the ground build; `reshapeGround` patches it incrementally at runtime.
- **Ground wear and PATHS**: path polylines are routed in plan space (§4) then *painted into the ground's colour/wear attributes* (`pathWearAt`, `yardWearAt`, `groundWear` in the bake; PATH_HUBS computed index.html:3932) — heights don't move paths (they're {x,z}), but the *wear* showing them is baked with the terrain colours, so a re-bake must re-run path painting or the trails vanish from the ground (they'd still exist as data).
- Foliage chunks: tuft positions are world-baked from `sampleHeight` at placement (MU-13 §2.6) — a height change buries/floats every tuft until re-scattered/re-baked.
- Trees and rocks: placed by `sampleHeight` (scatter rules read slope/height/moisture, MU-14 §1), then their batch cells are **world-space copies** (MU-13 §2.4/2.5) — a height change leaves both the live trees (seated at old y) and the batch copies floating/buried until re-scatter + re-batch.
- POI placement (`planPOIs/findSpot`, MU-12 §1): positions are {x,z} chosen *by* height rules (slope caps, water checks) — a new height function re-rolls which spots qualify (same seed, different acceptance), so every POI, camp, site anchor and tied mission spot (MU-12 §4 lists) moves.
- Objective props: `objectiveSitesFrom(POI, height)` takes y from the height at build (MU-12 §1); props seat on lowest-of-footprint ground.
- History props: seated by `sampleHeight` at placement (MU-12 §4), including the ranger truck's wheel-plane seating; camps' grading: camp/cabin builds take `gy` from the ground at build (`buildCampsite` index.html:5631; cabins `groundMinAround`), tent decks level from that — all cached y.
- Colliders: `addSolid` y0/y1 are baked from ground at build; platforms/decks (`gradeApproach` deckY) likewise; cave mouths, the cellar excavation and the heightField depression under it.
- The minimap: baked from the world (terrain colours + POIs) at load — it repaints markers live but its ground image is a bake.
- Water surfaces: lake level is a constant, but the *shoreline* is where height meets it — a height change moves shores without touching the water code; river ribbon follows `riverBedDense` (solved from `terrainBase`, §1 step 2).

**Cached positions that would float or bury (the dangerous list):** every placed mesh's y (landmarks, props, history props, camps), tree/rock batch world copies (they are *copies* — even re-seating live trees doesn't fix the batches; `buildTreeBatches`/`buildRockBatches` must re-run), foliage chunk vertices, the FOB batch + landmark batch world copies (MU-13), cave hill domes (height function *of* the caves: `caveHillHeight` adds on top of base — a base change shifts the mouths' aprons), the trapper yard's whole local frame (`cy0` + cap grid snapshot, game/trapper-path.js), ` POI.caves` mouths' `gy`, bridge/dock deckY + their graded approaches, and saved games: any persisted position (objectives' saved state stores ids/states, not heights — good; survivor/roof/bounty positions are runtime, not saved — good).

## 3. So: a height-function change is this list

1. Re-run: river bed solve → heightField bake → apron flatten → cave hills → approaches — in load order (they're sequential build steps; there is no single "rebuild terrain" entry point to call, which is the first thing W1 should consider adding if heights will be tuned iteratively).
2. Rebuild: terrain mesh + wedges, ground colours/wear (incl. PATHS painting), minimap ground.
3. Re-scatter/re-seat: foliage, trees (+ batches), rocks (+ batches), POIs (which re-rolls sites/camps/missions per MU-12 §4), props, history props, colliders, decks.
4. Re-check: tests that read heights (the trapper/cellar suites, cave suites, bench seats, t-tests that stand the marine somewhere specific) and CU-196's sightlines (W1's own test: with new ridges, "no place open from the yard" is measured, not assumed).
What rebuilds *by itself*: everything in §2's live-reader group, plus the flow field (next rebuild), plus water depth/shores (they're derived per point). Nothing that owns a mesh rebuilds by itself.

**For W1 specifically:** a directional ridge/hollow layer in `terrainBase` (step 1) is the cheap injection point — one place, before the river solve — but §3's full list is the price per iteration. That argues for W1 tuning heights through AG/Claude seats with the sightline tool in the loop, not for many small height commits. Clearings (§4 of MU-14) pair with this: a clearing is flatten + scatter-exclusion at a site; the apron (index.html:3219) is the existing prototype — `flattenBuildApron` generalised per-site (centre, radius, feather, river-keep) is the shape, and sites' POIs are known before the flatten would need to run (planPOIs is solved before the ground bakes — the apron precedent proves the order works).

## 4. PATHS: routing, wear, and where a bending trail is made

**Routing (all inside `planPOIs`, after POI positions are solved):** the network is a tree of legs. `ROOT` is the HQ at (0,0). Each destination (campsites, dock, graveyard, tower *ladder foot*, sheds[0], wrecks[0], mast — sorted nearest-first, index.html:3943ff) joins via `joinNetwork` (index.html:3876): cheapest junction over all existing leg vertices, cost = `cum × TRAIL_EASE (0.55)` + new distance — walking existing trail is 45% cheaper, which is what makes trunks and forks instead of a starburst — rejecting any leg that fords the river or wades the lake (bridges are crossed only via their deck legs, `crossAt`, index.html:3918; a destination on the far bank routes via the nearest bridge). Junctions split legs (`splitLeg`), traffic counts widen the shared stretches (`line.hw = TRAIL_HW + 0.3 × min(4, traffic−1)`, index.html:3970ff).

**The bend is already here:** each new leg is `wavyLeg` (index.html:3820) — points every ~3.5 m, bowed sideways by two octaves of fbm (`maxBow` up to 5.5 m or 20% of leg length), tapered straight at both ends, keyed per leg by `pathTag += 23.7` so other legs' bends never shift when one is added (pathTag determinism is load-bearing — `roundTowerLeg`'s comment at index.html:3900 relies on it). The tower's spur additionally rounds the tower at 4.5 m to reach the ladder (`roundTowerLeg`).

**Wear:** legs publish to `PATHS` as {x,z} polylines with half-width; the ground bake paints them via `pathWearAt` (+ `PATH_HUBS` where 2+ trails share an endpoint, index.html:3932) into the terrain's worn colours; foliage scatter avoids them (`distToPath < 2.2` keeps trees off, MU-14) and `distToPath` also serves wildlife/burrows.

**Where a *more*-bending trail (W1: trails that bend to hide the way) is made:** the control points are all in `wavyLeg` — bow amplitude is capped by `Math.min(5.5, len × 0.2)` and the fbm frequencies (0.04/0.011). Raising the cap or adding a deliberate S (a waypoint offset perpendicular at t≈0.5, per leg, seeded by the same pathTag) bends trails without touching routing, wear or determinism. What it must respect: the no-ford/no-wade rejection happens on the *straight* join (index.html:3876) — a strongly bowed leg can bow *into* the river the straight line avoided; a bowed leg near a clearing can bow through the site it serves. W1's sightline goal (the trail shouldn't point at the place) is a *routing* input, not a wavyLeg input: `joinNetwork`'s cost could add a penalty for legs whose final approach bearing runs straight at the destination from far off (forcing the fork point closer, so only the last short spur aims at the site). Both changes are local to these two functions; everything downstream (wear, wanderers on PATHS, distToPath readers) follows unchanged.
