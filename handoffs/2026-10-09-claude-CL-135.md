## What the xylophone was

Every metal sound in CL-134 (the slide, the bolt, the links, the AR's buffer spring, every reload part, the brass,
dry fire) was "modal metal": five or six pure sine waves at fixed ratios ringing for 6 to 45 ms. That is how a
struck bar works, so it sounded like one: a spectrogram of a CL-134 reload shows clean horizontal lines, notes. The
handling's dull thuds were short sine sweeps at 200 to 600 Hz: a wood block ("bones clinking"). The bullet impacts
on stone and wood, and the grenade, were still the old triangle-wave tones, and the weapon wheel ticked with the
sound of a spent case landing (a ringing one).

## What it is now

- **Nothing is a sine but the low end.** The thump and sub of every gun (and the explosions) are CL-134's, layer for
  layer; t223 checks they are untouched. Everything above them is shaped noise, the way real foley is:
  - `clank`: steel on steel. A fraction of a millisecond of noise (the strike) through about twenty narrow bands
    scattered at random over a range (every variant its own), each ringing one to three milliseconds, the low ones
    dying first, over the dull knock of the part it belongs to. There is no pitch in it to hear.
  - `knock`: a thud with no metal in it (a palm on a magazine, polymer, a mag in the dirt, the wooden forend).
  - `scrape`: a slide racked, a mag pushed up the well, a bolt run back: grainy noise through a moving band.
  - `rattle`: a run of small clanks (the Watchman's belt links, a revolver's pawl ticking as the cylinder spins down,
    the AR's buffer settling).
  - `static`: a CRT's snow (below).
- **The guns' high end**: the action behind each shot (pistol slide, Wasp's open bolt, M4 bolt and buffer, AK's
  heavier bolt, Watchman's bolt and links, Breacher's gas system, revolver hammer, turrets' feed) is clank and rattle
  now. The Wasp's "bark" was narrow enough to be a note too: widened. Levels are matched to CL-134 so nothing got
  quieter; a suppressed gun keeps its CL-134 loudness (`supLevel`).
- **The reloads**: all 28 handling sounds rebuilt from those layers: a mag release button and latch, the mag
  scraping out, the new one sliding up and clicking home, a palm slap, the slide or charging handle back and slammed
  forward, the AK's rock, the drum, the bolt (up, back, forward, locked), the box latch and belt, shells into the
  tube, the cylinder, the launcher's tube. The ones that were still the old beeps are banks too now: the hand to the
  pouch, the flamer's valve (a turn and a hiss), its tank (unlatch, a heavy knock), its piezo igniter (two snaps and
  a soft catch), the rotary gun's motor (a gear whirr), and the settle at the end of a reload. Spent cases land in
  the dirt (a bright tick, a quick shimmer, a bounce), not on a glockenspiel.
- **Bullets landing** (they play all the time while you shoot): stone cracks and throws chips, steel clangs (a
  dense, short clang), wood thocks and splinters, dirt thuds and patters, water slaps and sprays, sand hisses; the
  odd ricochet off stone and steel is a band of noise whining down. The grenade's pin and spoon, and its bounce.
- **Switching weapons is a CRT changing channel.** `AudioSys.weaponSwitch()`: the dial's detent, about 150 ms of
  snow flickering frame by frame with the 60 Hz buzz under it and crackle in it, cut dead, the picture popping on.
  It plays on every change (number keys, Q, the wheel, the holster key), once each. The wheel moving
  over a gun ticks the dial (`wheelTick()`: a detent and a blip of snow); so do the build wheel's pages and the
  build rotate key, which also used the case-landing sound.
- **The sniper's bolt** follows GB-146's new animation: up as it lifts, back and forward as each stroke starts,
  locked down at the end, and not after the last round (the reload works it then). It was baked into the shot at
  fixed times before.

## Levels

Matched to CL-134 (Jerry made no complaint about levels): the guns by loudness, the handling 0.4 of the way to its
CL-134 loudness and 0.6 to its peak (a short tick has a higher peak for its loudness than a ring had), then 1 dB
down; the impacts near the old ones'; the switch about -41 LUFS (clearly heard, well under a shot). One thing found
on the way: the offline render harness ramps in over its first ~0.1 s, so CL-134's calibration renders (all at
t = 0) read short sounds up to 5 dB low. The review renders for this card start at 0.4 s and are right.

## Tests

- **t223** (new, 15 checks): 49 handling, reload, impact, grenade and switch calls start no oscillator and each plays
  its own bank; no bank rings like a struck bar (the measure hears a 3 kHz ringing sine at 1.00; the worst bank, the
  ammo box seating, is 0.48 against a 0.5 bar); no layer is the old modal metal or spring; the switch is ~150 ms of
  broadband, flickering static, then cut; two weapon changes are two channel changes, holster and draw one each; a
  sniper round brings the bolt's four sounds once each and the last round none; the low end is CL-134's.
