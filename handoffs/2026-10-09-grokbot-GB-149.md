# GB-149 - Legs keep up with the upper body on turns (Jerry, 2026-10-09 03:37 CT)

Jerry: "the top half of the players body moves faster than his legs can keep up on rotation ... the legs need to keep up more with the top half."
Visual only: aim, movement speed, controls and hitboxes are untouched (t226 checks the facing equals the aim after every turn).

Cause: standing, GP-159's turn steps let the hips and feet lag the chest with no limit (up to 180 deg on a snap; 0.55 s to catch up with a rifle). Moving, POSE.lowerYaw eased at a flat rate of 9, and with a rifle the legs face the strafe direction (up to 0.85 rad) while the chest is bladed 0.8 rad the other way: 105 deg of twist the whole time you strafe right.

Changed:
- index.html updateMarinePose (Claude's area; Jerry assigned it to me; his blending is not rewritten): new TWIST_LIMIT = { cap: 0.96 (55 deg), hipYaw: 0.6, follow: 9, catchUp: 16, legEase: 14 }. The hips follow at 9 + 16*min(1, err/0.8), faster the further behind. Walking, the hips' target is held within 55 deg of the chest and the thighs take the rest of the travel direction as hip yaw (up to 34 deg; the legs' Euler order is YXZ, identical to XYZ at y = 0), so the boots still step along the travel line. A hard cap after the torso block keeps lowerBody within 55 deg of torsoG.
- studio/marine-turn.js (ChatGPT's GP-159/160): TWIST = { cap: .96, hip: .45, share: .5 }. The hips lead the planted feet by up to 26 deg; past cap + hip the feet pivot on the balls (steps.pivot, which re-aims a step in flight so it never lands past the aim). Steps that start far behind the aim are quicker (down to 70% of 0.20 s at 180 deg). Fixed three foot-yaw bugs the pivot exposed: the swing foot's fromQ/q weren't pivoted, a same-foot repeat step started from last frame's pose (drift), and a near-180 step turned the wrong way round (now measured from the support foot; standing boots turn by the step's own yaw).
- Why 55 deg: the rifle's bladed hold alone is 46 deg, and a back turns about 45-50 deg on the hips; a 45 deg cap would fight the hold. The rig has one waist joint, so the rest is spread to the pelvis (standing) and the hip joints (moving).
