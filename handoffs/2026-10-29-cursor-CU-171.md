## Method

The same night 20 High bench, `--prof-all`, on a tree of each kind. In every scratch tree the `zombies` mark is split at its three calls: `z:update` (updateZombies), `z:abilities` (enemyAbilities.update), `z:feral` (feralMotion.update); the three sum to the old `zombies` mark. Probes, on a snapshot of the current tree: `s` (tickShamblerRecovery forced to return false), `c` (game/screamer-behavior.js as in 82d75118, i.e. its applyScreamerPose), `p` (the GP-19979 spider block put back to its 82d75118 text: the lineSawBlock hold and the commit-in; the GB-241 shambler turn is not in it). Before each run: CPU % over 3 s, the five busiest processes and process counts, saved as `<run>.seat.json`.

## Reading it

1. MU-2's prediction that the three sub-marks sit within 0.05 ms of the old build is refuted for `z:update`: it is +0.34 to +0.48 ms, 4 of 4 new runs above the old mean in phase D. `z:abilities` and `z:feral` hold.
2. The three suspects named in MU-2's probe C do not account for it: shambler recovery off (D, 2 runs), the old screamer pose (C), the old spider block (C) all leave `z:update` where the current build is. MU-2 found only the spider block and the GB-241 line changed in `updateZombies` itself, and its helpers unchanged; so the growth is in state those read, or in something the diff missed. The next cut is a split inside `updateZombies` (marks per type or per stage), not a stub of another suspect.
3. About half of the tick growth is `r:render` and `r:matrices`, not the zombies' code: that is AG-151's draws by owner (probe B) and the scene's builders, not the zombies.
4. Any old-against-new comparison on this seat has to take the first-run effect out (a throwaway first, as in D), or it is wrong by about 3.5 ms of tick and 2 ms of `r:render`.
