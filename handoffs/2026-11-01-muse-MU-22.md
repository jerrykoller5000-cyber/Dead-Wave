## 1. The collider system

- **One shape only: the circle.** `addSolid(x, z, radius, y0, y1, landmark = null)` (index.html:3518) pushes `{x, z, radius, y0, y1, landmark}` onto `worldSolids` (declared index.html:3502: "blocks movement, builds, bullets") and returns the record (callers keep it to remove later — removal is `worldSolids.indexOf/splice` everywhere, e.g. doors index.html:30109, utility props index.html:32657). There is **no segment/box solid** in worldSolids: long things are chains of circles (cave walls at 1.3–1.6 m spacing, index.html:8057; fallen logs at 0.8 m, MU-20). Boxes exist only in the *build* system (`pushOutOfBuild`'s thin/link boxes, §2) and platforms (`{x, z, hw, hl, yaw, deckY}`).
- **y0/y1 matter:** a solid only pushes bodies whose feet plausibly overlap its span (resolveStaticProps: `footY > ws.y1 − 0.3` → skip; `footY + 2.2 < ws.y0` → skip, index.html:55257ff). A wall band must set y0/y1 from the ground honestly or flyers/deck-walkers will be shoved by air (the bridge-rail bug in that function's comments is the cautionary tale, and the flow-field test caught it).
- **The spatial lookup:** `propsNear(x, z, r)` (index.html:55224) over `propGrid` — 8 m cells (`PROP_CELL`, index.html:55208), filing trees (trunk solids), rocks and worldSolids (`fileProp` files an object into every cell its radius touches). The grid **rebuilds at most every 12 frames** (stamp includes the three lengths, index.html:55224ff), so a solid added/removed can take up to 12 frames to reach collision — fine for walls, worth knowing for gates. Query cost is O(objects in the 3×3-ish cells touched), independent of total count. A second, older pattern coexists: raw `for (const ws of worldSolids)` linear scans at ~a dozen call sites (spawn-clearance, relay/ranger placement, `overlapsBlocked` index.html:35487) — those are build/event-time, not per-frame, except where noted in §4.
- `caveSealSolids` (index.html:8302) are the precedent for *him-only* blocking (the Marrow door: "against him only (the dead pass: they are not worldSolids)") — a separate list consulted by his movement only. MU-18's iron needs the mirror image (dead-only), which the navigation grid gives without touching solids at all.

## 2. Who meets solids, and how

- **Him:** movement resolves through `resolveStaticProps(px, pz, radius, y)` then build collision (`resolveHardBuildCollisions`/`pushOutOfBuild` per nearby build: decks stood on, stairs as wedges, doors solid until opened *for anyone*, thin/link boxes, else circle — index.html:55323ff) plus `resolveTreeCollisions` and the house push-out (`pushOutOfHouse` inside resolveStaticProps). Slopes/step limits are separate (MU-14).
- **The dead:** the **same** `resolveStaticProps` per zombie per frame (called from updateZombies with the zombie's radius), plus the same build push-outs via their movement code, plus separation. This symmetry is why "the dead and he both blocked" is the default for anything in worldSolids — a wall needs no zombie-side code at all.
- **The horde's slots/approach points:** slots are geometry round the target (MU-14); they meet solids *indirectly*: `hordeOpenWay` vetoes through the flow field, and bodies are pushed out after the step. A slot can still be *picked* inside a wall's circle and abandoned — the field veto is the only prevention today (MU-18 §6 proposes generalising it).
- **Thrown things:** grenades/arc projectiles test ground and builds; a wall of solids gives them collision through the same radius tests the projectile step uses for props — a thrown thing over a 2 m wall clears it by height (solids' y1 is the gate: set a thicket's y1 at its real top, ~2–2.5 m, and grenades arc over, which is presumably wanted).
- **Bullets:** hitscan/projectile steps test landmark solids to find hp pools (the `landmark` tag exists for this: "a bullet or the tower siege can find its way back to an hp pool", index.html:3514ff) and stop at solid scenery. **A wall band should pass no landmark (indestructible scenery) or a chosen one (a breachable wall with hp) — that single argument decides whether the dead/he can ever shoot through the thicket line.** Flagged for re-check: I summarised the bullet path from registration, not from the step code.
- **The flow field (GB-264's blocked source):** reads worldSolids + trees + rocks + builds into blocked cells (MU-14 §2) — so anything added as solids *automatically* walls the dead's routing too, with `FF_CLEAR` inflation. Collision and navigation already agree by construction; a wall band that registers solids inherits both.

## 3. How tryPlace tests for room

`placeRefusal` (MU-14 §3) → for ground pieces, `overlapsBlocked(x, z, radius)` (index.html:35487): **linear scans** over all trees, all rocks, all worldSolids, platforms, house — O(everything) per call, called per ghost move (mouse-rate, one footprint at a time — acceptable today at hundreds of solids; §4 prices it at thousands). "Clear ground only" (MU-14 §3) lands in the same function; a wall band adds one more refused overlap (his buildings can't straddle the thicket line — or *can* they? A wall built into the ring closing a gap is presumably a feature: the placeRefusal rule should refuse building **on** the band's line except… no: simplest honest rule, refuse on the band, gaps stay gaps until *he* walls them with builds, which the builds system already handles).

## 4. What a long wall of solids costs

Sizing a real wall: an HQ ring at, say, 55–70 m radius is 350–440 m of perimeter; W2's bands elsewhere (across valleys between woods) are shorter. At log-chain spacing (0.8 m) a full ring is **~450–550 solids**; at cave spacing (1.6 m, radius ~0.8) ~250. Say **+300–600 worldSolids** on top of today's few hundred.

- **Collision (per frame):** ~unchanged in cost. `propsNear` is grid-local; a zombie *at* the wall tests the solids filed in its 3×3 cells — a dense chain files ~2 per metre, so a cell holds ~16 wall solids and the query tests maybe 30–50 circles instead of today's ~10. At 48 zombies + him, worst case (all pressed on the wall) ~2,500 extra circle tests a frame — noise (each is a few flops with the AABB early-out at index.html:55257). The 12-frame grid stamp means the cost of *rebuilding* the grid grows with total solids (filing 600 extra records every 12 frames — still trivial).
- **`overlapsBlocked` and the linear scans:** these grow linearly with worldSolids — +600 records on a mouse-move-rate call is fine; the same scans at *placement-test* call sites during world build (history props, relay spots: each candidate × all solids) grow too, all at load. Acceptable.
- **The flow field:** blocked-marking cost grows per rebuild (0.4 s cadence): each solid rasterises ~ (2r+0.9)²/1.25² ≈ 4–9 cells → +600 solids ≈ +4k cell-writes per rebuild. Trivial against 16k-cell Dijkstra (MU-18 §5).
- **Memory/save:** 600 small objects; solids are not saved (rebuilt at load) — a wall band must be rebuilt deterministically at load like logs aren't (logs are runtime). Fine.
- **The real cost is not CPU, it's tuning:** circle chains leak at joins (a body can wedge between two circles on a curve and jitter), and 0.8 m spacing with radius < spacing leaves scalloped gaps a thin zombie (radius 0.35) can nose through at the diagonal — the field's no-corner-cut rule won't save collision. Spacing ≤ radius is the safe chain (logs use 0.8 spacing with radius trunk+0.2 ≈ 0.55+ — scalloped but trunk-thick; a designed wall should use spacing ≤ 0.7× diameter, i.e. overlap the circles).

## 5. Where a wall-band primitive best sits

**Proposal: a `wallBands` registry beside worldSolids, not instead of it.** A band = `{ pts: [{x,z}…] (a polyline), halfWidth, y0off, topOff, gaps: [{at (arc length), width}], kind }`. On registration (and on any gap change) it **generates its solids** into worldSolids (circles along each segment at ≤0.7×diameter spacing, skipping gap spans) and keeps the generated list so a gap opening/closing regenerates only its span. Everything downstream — his collision, the dead's, tryPlace, the flow field, bullets — works *unchanged*, because they all already read worldSolids. What the band adds over hand-placed circles:

- **Gaps as data:** a gap is a span with a name/state (open / ironed / gated / log-blocked) — MU-18's field events and MU-20's log cuts address *the gap*, not a pile of circles. Iron (dead-only) lives in the field, not in the band's solids (or a gap the player can walk but the dead can't becomes impossible — it must NOT generate solids; solids block both).
- **One authoring shape** for thicket/log/rock bands (they differ in mesh and top height, not in collision): W2's builders emit bands; the meshes are Antigravity/Grokbot's; collision is one generator.
- **Deterministic rebuild at load:** bands are defined from POIs/trails (the HQ ring "open only where trails pass" = ring polyline with gaps *computed from PATHS crossings* — the trail network is solved before world build, MU-16 §4, so the gap list is derivable, not hand-placed: every PATH crossing the ring radius becomes a gap of trail width + margin). That derivation is the piece to prototype first; it is what keeps CU-205-style POI moves from orphaning the ring (recompute gaps from the new trails, regenerate).
- Where it sits in code: next to `addSolid`/worldSolids (index.html:3502), a `registerWallBand` + `bandSetGap(band, gapId, state)` pair, called from the world build after PATHS exist; the TT surface gets `wallBands()` for tests. The proof test is the flow-field suite's own shape: marine on one side, zombie on the other, only the gap passable — he walks the gap, the dead route to it, a body pressed mid-band doesn't leak (the scallop test), and opening/closing a gap moves the dead's route on the next field rebuild.

**Alternatives rejected:** segment solids as a new shape in resolveStaticProps (touches every resolver and the grid filing for zero gain — circles at overlapped spacing are equivalent at these widths); one giant radius per band arc (blocks the whole disc sector, gaps impossible); builds-system walls (they're his, destructible, Cash-priced — the world's walls are scenery).
