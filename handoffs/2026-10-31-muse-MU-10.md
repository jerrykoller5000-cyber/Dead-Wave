## The answer for CL-283 (J36: "a sound like rattling chains when the player walks or runs")

There is no chain, gear, sling or dog-tag layer to remove: no code plays a separate gear/foley sound on the gait cycle (§1). The chain-like sound, if it is in the walk/run loop at all, is inside the footstep takes themselves. By spectrum the most chain-like single takes in the catalogue are the pack's metal boots (`pk-ocular-boot-metal-01..04`: one narrow ringing high partial each, 162–888× the HF median — a true clink), but those play only on metal ground (HQ roof, tower deck, trapper's cellar), not on an ordinary walk. On ordinary ground the takes that actually play by default are the pack's gravel steps (walk) and gravel stomps (run) — the brightest dirt takes in the set, with ringing gravel ticks — repeated once per footfall; that repetition is the only "rattle" the walk/run loop can make. The loudest high-frequency takes overall (the leaves set, 47–92 % of energy above 4 kHz) measure as continuous crunch, not clinks, and play only under a tree canopy. Details and the full table: §3–§4 and the appendix.

## 1. How a step becomes a sound (the code's calls)

One trigger, one sound per footfall. In the movement update, the gait cycle's strike crossing (or a distance fallback: every 2.0 m walking, 2.5 m running, 1.5 m crouched) sets `fell`, and exactly one call fires:

- On boards (a floor, platform, stairs, bridge or dock — `onBoards()`, [index.html](/mnt/c/Users/Zero/Desktop/Tiny Trek/index.html:3451)): `AudioSys.footstepWood(moveRunning)` ([index.html](/mnt/c/Users/Zero/Desktop/Tiny Trek/index.html:60139)).
- Otherwise: `AudioSys.footstep(depth > 0.06, moveRunning, stepGround())` ([index.html](/mnt/c/Users/Zero/Desktop/Tiny Trek/index.html:60140)), where `stepGround()` ([index.html](/mnt/c/Users/Zero/Desktop/Tiny Trek/index.html:3457)) returns `'stone'` below ground, `'metal'` on the HQ roof / tower deck / in the trapper's cellar, `'leaves'` under a live tree's canopy, else `null`.
- A hard landing reuses the same two calls with `running = true` ([index.html](/mnt/c/Users/Zero/Desktop/Tiny Trek/index.html:60380)).
- In Training, `api.footstep` is rerouted to `trainingCue('step')` ([audio.js](/mnt/c/Users/Zero/Desktop/Tiny Trek/core/audio.js:3868)), whose take is `player/footstep-tile` ([audio.js](/mnt/c/Users/Zero/Desktop/Tiny Trek/core/audio.js:3571)).

Inside `footstep(wet, running, ground)` ([audio.js](/mnt/c/Users/Zero/Desktop/Tiny Trek/core/audio.js:1012)): wet (wading, depth > 0.06) → `player/footstep-water`; else ground `stone|metal|leaves` → `player/footstep-<ground>`; else running → `player/footstep-run-dirt`, walking → `player/footstep-dirt`. Volume 1 running, 0.85 walking. `footstepWood(running)` ([audio.js](/mnt/c/Users/Zero/Desktop/Tiny Trek/core/audio.js:3440)) → `player/footstep-wood`, same volumes. If a folder has no playable take (not loaded, switched off, empty), a synth fallback plays instead: a lowpassed noise thud plus a 2.6 kHz band tick for `footstep`, a sine knock plus noise (15 % chance of a board creak) for `footstepWood`. The folders are on the early-preload list ([takes.js](/mnt/c/Users/Zero/Desktop/Tiny Trek/core/takes.js:163)), so with takes present the synth is a load-time edge case, not the walk sound.

Searched for a gear layer and found none: no `gear`, `rattle`, `sling` (audio sense) or dog-tag call exists in the movement path — `grep -n "rattle|dogTag|sling" index.html core/audio.js` returns only weapon-carry geometry and the rotary gun's barrel rattle. The only other sound tied to running is the winded breath: `AudioSys.playerBreath(wd, half)` on each breath turn once `POSE.winded > 0.25` ([index.html](/mnt/c/Users/Zero/Desktop/Tiny Trek/index.html:20979); `WINDED.sound` [index.html](/mnt/c/Users/Zero/Desktop/Tiny Trek/index.html:20134)), taking `player/breath-tired` when well winded ([audio.js](/mnt/c/Users/Zero/Desktop/Tiny Trek/core/audio.js:930)). It is a breath, not a rattle, and it sounds while he stands or walks off a run, not per step.

## 3. The chain hunt: by name, then by spectrum

**By name.** Eleven of the 64 files announce metal or glass: the whole `footstep-metal` folder (3 his + 4 pack) and the tile folder's pack takes (`pk-ocular-boot-glass-01..04`). No file in any walk/run folder is named for chains, clinks, jingles, bells or keys — the chain-like quality, if present, is in the recording, not the label.

**By spectrum (numpy, offline).** Method (scripts in §Tests; STFT 2048/512, Hann): per file — energy-weighted spectral centroid, share of energy above 4 kHz and 8 kHz, HF transient count (sensitive pass), HF envelope crest, and HF ringing (peak/median of the mean HF spectrum: a narrow ringing partial scores tens to hundreds, broadband crunch scores single digits). A chain/jingle percept needs discrete, repeated, ringing metal transients; continuous HF noise (leaf crunch, splash) is bright without being chain-like, so the verdict uses HF share + transients + ringing together, never HF share alone. Headline measurements:

- **No file jingles.** Under the strict onset threshold no take has more than 2 discrete HF onsets (one footfall is one hit); the sensitive pass finds 1–12 small transients everywhere, splash and crunch included. Whatever Jerry hears as a rattle must be built by repetition — one bright tick per footfall, at step rate — not by a jingle inside a single take.
- **The true clinks are the pack's metal boots.** `pk-ocular-boot-metal-01..04`: HF ringing 162–888× (the set's extreme), one sparse clink per file (sparsity 0.74–0.92), yet dull in body (centroid 366–651 Hz, HF>4k ≤ 2.1 %) — a boot thud with a metal ring on top. His metal takes ring less (24–109×). All gated to metal ground (§1), so they explain J36 only if Jerry was walking the roof, the tower or the cellar.
- **The everyday suspects are the pack's gravel takes.** `pk-ocular-gravel-step-01..04` (walk) and `pk-ocular-gravel-stomp-01..04` (run) are 4–8× brighter than his dirt takes (centroids 752–1,900 Hz vs 30–263 Hz; HF>4k 2.3–11.9 % vs ≤1.0 %) with ringing ticks (25–124×) and bursty envelopes (sparsity up to 0.99). These are the takes that play by default on ordinary ground (§2), once per footfall, walking and running — the only bright, ticking sound the normal walk/run loop makes. His own dirt/run takes are dull LF thuds by every measure and cannot read as chains.
- **Brightest overall, but not chains: leaves.** His `footstep-leaves-1..4` carry 55–92 % of energy above 4 kHz (centroids 5.9–8.2 kHz — the set's highest) but ring at only 3–7×: continuous broadband crunch with no partial to ring. The pack's grass steps are similar (47–58 % HF, ring 25–48×). Canopy-gated in any case.
- **The rest are clean.** Wood (HF ≈ 0 %), stone/concrete (HF ≤ 0.4 %), his water and dirt takes, and the unreachable mud set show no chain-like signature. Two bright exceptions, both gated: `pk-ocular-boot-water-03` (HF 50.9 %, wading only) and `pk-ocular-boot-glass-02` (HF 49.5 %, ring 44×, Training only).

**What this means for CL-283's "find the layer".** There is no layer to mute: the fix is at take level. First suspects for Jerry's ear, in order: (1) `pk-ocular-gravel-stomp-*` and `pk-ocular-gravel-step-*` (default dirt walk/run — try the folder's "mine" switch, or a low-pass/level trim on the pack takes; his takes underneath are dull and safe); (2) `pk-ocular-boot-metal-*` if the complaint was on the roof/tower/cellar; (3) the leaves set if it was under trees. The synth fallback's 2.6 kHz tick is the only other bright element in the loop and sounds only while takes load.

## Appendix: method in one paragraph

Files measured: every `*.ogg` in the nine `z-sounds/player/footstep-*` folders (64 files; `_prompt.txt` excluded). Decode: soundfile (libsndfile, Vorbis) to mono float; STFT 2048-sample Hann, 512 hop. Centroid = energy-weighted mean frequency; HF shares = Σmag² above 4/8 kHz over total; transients = positive spectral-flux peaks in the >4 kHz band (strict pass: >25 % of band max and >6× median flux; sensitive pass: >4× median flux); crest = max/mean of the HF band envelope; sparsity = share of HF energy in the loudest 20 % of frames; ring = max/median of the mean magnitude spectrum above 4 kHz. Full per-file numbers were printed by the two scripts in Tests (kept under /tmp, not committed); §4 carries every flagged file and every folder's range.
