## 1. How an attack is timed today (the warnings already implicit)

- **The standard swing:** in reach and off cooldown, a zombie sets `attackWindup = 0.45` (index.html:56860); 0.45 s later the hit lands if he's still within reach + 0.4, `swingT = 0.28` plays the arm, cooldown 1.1 s (index.html:56853ff). So there *is* a windup — just under half a second, with the attack voice at its start (below). Animation-side, the body reads it: `B.wind` ramps over 0.22 s (index.html:46848; reach grows 0.35 → 1 with wind) — a visible tell *if the zombie is in his light*.
- **The lunge (GB-94/248):** horde zombies in the 3–6 m band (HORDE.LUNGE_IN/OUT) start a lunge (`LUNGE_S` duration, index.html:57909) at lunge speed (shambler 5.0, soldier 6.0 m/s, scaled by slows; blood moon +20%) — closing 3–6 m in ~0.6–1 s. **The feral coils first: 0.18 s dropped into its knees** (index.html:57906/58997; GB-248 made the coil *because* the lunge needed a tell), then springs; recovery 0.24 s after. Others lunge with no coil — speed itself is the only warning.
- **The leaper:** its leap has a voice state (`'leaper', 'leap'` — growl 700→1500 Hz, index.html core/audio.js:3316) and its move-voice chirps as it comes (core/audio.js:3259). The leap itself is fast; the *sound* is the warning.
- **The feral generally:** coil + leap voice + the fastest closure; today its fairness rests on those two tells.
- **From the dark specifically:** a zombie outside torch/NVG light is visible only by eyes/muzzle flash context and audible cues (§2). The windup/coil tells are *visual* — at night beyond the light they don't exist for the player. That gap is precisely W3.4.

## 2. What he hears before one reaches him (today)

- **Voices, positional:** `updateZombies`' sound pass calls `AudioSys.zombieVoice(type, 'attack', v, pan, z)` exactly at windup (index.html:56155) — so a swing is *announced* 0.45 s early, in the voice of its kind (core/audio.js:3293ff: per-kind growls/breaths; chase states get chase growls, breath sequences). `'move'` voices sound while kinds move (leaper chirps every third step; colossus has its own step voice, core/audio.js:3259–3260). Volume v is the caller's distance curve (quiet far, present near) — I did not re-derive its exact metres this session; it is the same family as the footstep curve MU-10 mapped for the player's own steps.
- **Footsteps of the dead:** the audio engine's per-kind step sounds (core/audio.js zombie step block, ~3250ff: kinds have distinct steps; the colossus stamps). A walking zombie in the open is audible well before contact on a quiet night — *in the open*. **In brush, nothing changes the sound**: there is no surface/vegetation parameter on zombie steps (the player's steps have surfaces, MU-10; the dead's steps do not read foliage).
- **The brush itself is silent for the dead:** foliage chunks trample-bend for bodies (the foliage material's tramplers, index.html, GP-19089's player trampling; zombies are registered tramplers too — visual only), trees don't rustle when brushed, branches don't snap. Ground fires crackle, wind and crickets play (packs below) — the night woods bed is *ambient*, not *reactive*.
- **The shape crossing the light:** flashlight/NVG reveal is binary visibility; there's no rim/glint telegraph. The muzzle-flash and hit-flash are his own. So today, an attack from the dark is warned by: voice-at-windup (0.45 s, any direction, pan tells side), chase growls on approach (kind-dependent, only in chase state and gate-limited — `createZombieVoiceGate`, core/audio.js:770 throttles per kind/state so a horde doesn't chorus), and footsteps (open ground). **A sleeping/wandering zombie that wakes close, or a quiet kind closing through brush, can be on him with only the 0.45 s voice** — that is the cheap case W3.4 exists to remove.

## 3. What the packs already hold (branches, brush, leaves, footfalls)

From the catalogue scan (docs/audio/packs/*.json, name + "why" fields):

- **Player footstep on leaves/grass exists:** `grass-step-01…04` (docs/audio/packs/player-builds.json, `player/footstep-leaves`) — "Dry grass is the rustle; it is not a twig crunch" — Ocular Stride source, chosen for the *player's* steps (MU-10 lists the full surface set). Four variants, dry rustle. These are the only undergrowth footfalls in the packs.
- **Branch snapping for a zombie: nothing.** No branch/twig/snap take is catalogued for vegetation (the "snap" hits are a guardian's "snapping lunge" *description*, a caveguard chitter description, and weapon foley). **Brush rustling (a body pushing through): nothing.** Leaves: the wind bed (`world/wind-day` — "wind through trees and leaves", docs/audio/packs/world.json) and night crickets with leaves in light wind (`world/crickets-night`) — ambience, not an event.
- So a warning build needs either (a) reuse of `grass-step` takes at zombie positions (wrong owner-feel: they're *his* steps' sound; using them for the dead would teach the player to ignore his own step sound — flagged, not recommended), or (b) **new takes**: a branch snap (short, woody crack, 0.1–0.2 s) and a brush pass (0.4–0.8 s leaves/brush noise swell) recorded/licensed into the z-sounds pack (ChatGPT owns pack curation; each file needs its source/licence line like the others). The engine side already supports positional one-shots with pan/volume (`playTake` with emitter, used by `zombieVoice` throughout).

## 4. Where a warning hook would sit

The cleanest insertion is in `updateZombies`' per-zombie frame logic, beside the existing tells — one new per-zombie field (`z.warnedT` timestamp), checked cheaply:

1. **Trigger:** a zombie that is *hunting him* (chase/lunge intent — not asleep, not wandering: a warning for a sleeper is noise, not fairness) crosses **into ~15 m** of the player **while outside his effective light** (not flashlight-lit, not in the HQ flood pool, night or below-light conditions — reuse the same light tests the fade/darkness code uses, rather than a new darkness model), and hasn't warned in the last ~6 s.
2. **The sound:** placed *at the zombie* (emitter = z, as `zombieVoice` already does): a branch snap if it's moving fast/through trees (near a trunk — trees are data, MU-17: nearest-tree distance is a trees[] scan over near ones only, bounded like the other near scans), else a brush rustle if it's in foliage (foliage density/chunk presence at its cell is known to the foliage system), else **nothing** — open-ground approaches keep today's footsteps/growls; the rule stays "the *woods* warn", which is W3's text, not a proximity alarm. That conditionality is what keeps it fair-not-cheap: the warning tells him *something is in the brush that way*, not *a zombie is at 14 m*.
3. **The visual half:** "a rustle in the brush mesh near it": the foliage trample system already bends tufts for bodies — a warning could add a brief, stronger sway pulse at its position (the foliage material's trampler slot, time-limited) so a lit-edge zombie *stirs* the brush it is in. Cheap (one uniform write), and it dies with the same event. A shape crossing *the light itself* needs nothing: it's seen.
4. **Budget/discipline:** gated per zombie (warn once per approach; reset when it leaves 25 m or lands a hit) and globally rare — if three zombies approach through brush at once, three rustles from three directions is *correct* information, but the voice gate's lesson (core/audio.js:770) applies: cap concurrent warnings (~2/frame window) or a horde in the woods becomes a drum kit. The mix already ducks under gunfire; the hooks inherit that.
5. **Not the hook:** don't put it in `damagePlayer`'s path or the windup — 0.45 s is too late for the "from the dark" case; the warning's whole value is at 15 m (≈3–5 s of approach), giving him time to turn a light or step out.

**Cost honesty:** the check is one distance + one light test per *chasing* zombie per frame (the loop already computes both for other purposes — the distance is `hzPd`/d, the light test is the new part; reuse the per-frame zombie loop, no new scan). The foliage pulse is one uniform update. The data cost is two new takes in the z-sounds pack. MU-9's discipline applies: no allocation in the loop (reuse a scratch vector), and the warned test should run at the sound-pass cadence if the full-rate check ever shows on a profile.
