## 1. The proposal in one paragraph

Keep today's player field exactly as it is. Add **one valley-wide static field** — same 1.25 m cells, same Dijkstra, same `flowDirOn` reader, but centred on the HQ, covering the 220 m playable circle, seeded *at the HQ*, and rebuilt **only when the topology changes** (a wall built, a log cut, iron hung, a gate opened). A zombie far from the player walks the valley field downhill toward the HQ; inside the player field's range it hands over to today's fine steering unchanged. Run the valley field's rebuilds in a worker (the rebuild code is already pure typed-array work); the per-frame player field stays on the main thread as today, with the worker as its optional second step. Thicket is blocked, trails are cheap, open woods cost more than trail but are passable, water stays as today. Iron blocks cells **in the navigation grid only** — the player never collides with it, because the field is read only by the dead.

## 2. The grid

- **Cell size 1.25 m — deliberately the same as today** (`FF_CELL`, index.html:53952). Reasoning: the gaps are the design's unit. A trail gap through the thicket ring is 1.7–4 m wide (trail half-widths, MU-16 §4); at 1.25 m that is 1–3 open cells, and the field's no-corner-cutting rule (diagonal steps may not pass a blocked orthogonal neighbour) needs at least 2 open cells to route a ~0.9 m-wide body cleanly. At 2.5 m cells the same gap is a single cell and the no-corner-cut rule seals it. The finer grid is not a luxury; it is what makes "gaps where the trails pass" expressible at all.
- **Extent:** the playable circle is ~220 m (terrain wall band, MU-16 §1). A square grid of 384×384 cells at 1.25 m covers ±240 m — 147,456 cells. Memory per field: `dist` Float32 0.59 MB, `h` Float32 0.59 MB, `blocked`/`wet` 0.15 MB each — ~1.5 MB, nothing next to the tree/rock batch buffers.
- **Blocked cells, from:** thicket/log/rock wall bands (W2's new solids — rasterised as circles/segments the way `ffMarkCircle` already works), deep water (`wet` already separates wading from swimming; cells too deep for a walker stay blocked for non-aquatic types — today that is a steering-level check, so per-type fields are NOT proposed: blocked stays type-blind and the aquatic exception stays in local steering, as now), the HQ footprint, player builds (exact `pushOutOfBuild` test, as today), cave-mouth keep-outs if desired. **Trees are NOT marked in the valley field** (see §3).
- **Costs:** trail cell 0.6 (a visible incentive to use the ways), open ground 1.0, **woods 1.6** (cells under canopy / inside the forest scatter — from the same density data W2's building rule reads, MU-14 §3; a zombie *can* push through woods, slowly and indirectly, which is what makes gaps matter without making woods into walls), wet 2.5 (today's number), climb limit unchanged (`FF_CLIMB` 0.47/m — this is what makes ridges into walls for free where they're steep, MU-16's ridges included).

## 3. Why trees stay out of the valley field

Today's fine field marks every trunk (`ffMarkCircle` per tree in the grid). That is correct at 160 m (430 trees, up to ~100 in range, rebuilt 2.5×/s) and wrong at valley scale with W2's many-times forest: at, say, 3,000 trees it is 3,000 circle-rasterisations *per rebuild* and, worse, at 1.25 m cells a trunk inflated by `FF_CLEAR` (0.45) blocks a 2–3 cell blob — a dense forest would read as one blocked mass with no gaps, and the dead would never enter woods at all. But W2 *wants* the dead in the woods, following trails and gaps. So: the valley field prices woods (cost 1.6) instead of blocking trunks; the fine player field keeps marking trunks for the last 160 m where trunk-level avoidance matters; and thicket (the designed wall) is a different input from forest (the priced medium). This split — **walls block, woods cost** — is the proposal's one real design claim; Claude/Jerry to confirm it matches "terror by night" (the dead coming *through* the trees, slowly, is scarcer and better than the dead never entering them).

## 4. The fields and their rebuilds

**Field HQ (new, static-topology):** seeded at the HQ (seed ring outside its own blocked footprint — `updateFlowFieldOn`'s `seedR > 0` mode already exists for the house field, `ffHouse`, the Defend precedent). Rebuilt when, and only when:
- a player build is placed, damaged out, or removed (the builds list changes — hook the same events that already mark the fine field);
- a log is felled (already fires `dw-log`, which force-rebuilds the fine field today — extend the listener) or **cut through** (the chainsaw shortcut: log solid removed → cells unblock);
- iron is hung over or taken down from a gap (utilities `use('horseshoes')` moment, MU-15 §2);
- a story gate/blocker opens (CL-285 part C — a gate that blocks the *player* may or may not block the dead; the chain gates are player-blockers today, so default: they do **not** enter the field until a gate's owner says its blocker stops the dead too);
- the shovel reshapes ground (heights change — `reshapeGround` already knows its radius: update the cached `h` cells in that disc and rebuild);
- at run start and on reset. **Not** per frame, not per dawn. Between events the field is a constant; a zombie reading it allocates nothing (`flowDirOn` writes into a reused out-vector already).

**Field Player (today's, unchanged):** 160 m on him, 0.4 s rebuilds, trunk marking on. Its job shrinks in one way: it no longer has to be the *only* way round an obstacle for a zombie spawned 140 m away — the valley field delivers the zombie to its edge already on a sensible line.

**Hand-over:** a chasing zombie reads the valley field when (a) it is outside the player field's grid, or (b) the player field has no route from its cell (`dist === Infinity` — the existing escape case). It reads the player field inside range, as today. Defend's house field merges into this scheme unchanged (HQ field *is* a house field with valley extent; whether `ffHouse` is retired or kept as the near-HQ fine layer is Grokbot's detail — keeping it costs one more 16k-cell rebuild per 0.4 s during Defend only).

## 5. Cost: main thread against worker, with numbers

Estimate method: Dijkstra visits each cell once and relaxes ≤8 neighbours — ~147k pops + ~1M edge relaxations for the valley field; the heap is the same binary heap the fine field uses. JavaScript does roughly 50–150 M simple typed-array/heap ops/sec in this shape, so **a valley rebuild is ~10–25 ms of pure compute**, plus blocked-marking (walls/builds near the grid: small thousands of cells — negligible) plus heights (cached; only shovel discs resample). The fine field today is the same code over 16k cells — read `TT.flowField().ms` on Jerry's PC for the calibrated anchor; my estimate scales it ~9×.

- **On the main thread:** a 10–25 ms spike on a topology event. Events are rare and mostly player-caused (he *just* cut a log / hung iron / built a wall — a one-frame hitch under his own action, at prep, is barely visible; mid-wave, a build placement doing this inside the frame the dead are ticking could dent the 1% low the way the shadow redraw did, CL-237). With the *sliced* alternative (rebuild over ~4 frames), steering reads a half-built field — the fine field already tried and dropped slicing (t80 note in `updateFlowFieldOn`'s signature comment), so don't.
- **In a worker (recommended):** main-thread cost per rebuild ≈ serialising the *inputs*: wall/build/blocked-source snapshot. The clean message shape avoids sending trees at all (§3): `{type:'build', field:'hq', ox, oz, blocked: <transferred Uint8Array of source marks>, h?: <disc updates>}` — or simpler, the worker owns the static topology (walls, water, heights sampled once at init from the heightField it is *sent once* as a transferred Float32Array, 505×505 = 1 MB one-off) and receives only deltas: `{type:'topology', add:[{x,z,r}], remove:[...], builds:[...]}` then posts back `{type:'field', field:'hq', dist: <transferred Float32Array>, ox, oz, seq}`. Steering reads the last received field; staleness is bounded by "since the last topology event + one worker round trip (~10–30 ms)" — the dead notice a cut log one frame later than perfect, which no player can see. Fallback: if the worker fails to start (file:// worker restrictions are real for a no-build-step game — **a Worker from a Blob URL is the standard workaround; if even that is unavailable in Jerry's browser context, the same code runs on the main thread behind `?navworker=0`**), the main-thread path is the identical function. That fallback must exist from day one; the code is shared, only the caller differs. This is also CL-288 step 4's natural home: the fine field can move to the same worker later without re-proposing.

## 6. How updateZombies reads it (and what the horde becomes)

Minimal surgery, in the order the loop already computes things (MU-14 §2):

1. **Target selection unchanged** (player / smash / lure / window / defences / wanderer).
2. **Heading:** after the existing flow block, the valley field inserts *before* it: if the zombie's read of the player field is unavailable or routeless, `flowDirOn(ffValley, …)` supplies the heading (same easing into `z.flowX/Z`). Nothing else in the movement stack changes: horde slot headings still override when on a swung-out line (`hzSteer`), flank/weave/boss tactics keep their own steering, the bluff unstick sidestep stays as the local fallback for what any field misses (slopes between cells, a body shoved into a margin — the field's own escape case already exists).
3. **POI guards and wanderers** gain the most and change the least: a woken guard 140 m from the HQ currently direct-seeks; reading the valley field, it comes through the gaps. Wanderers stay on PATHS (they're *supposed* to be on the trail).
4. **The horde's slots/approach points** (`hordeOpenWay`, game/horde.js slots): keep the slot ring and approach-point maths — they make the last 20 m read as a pack. Two changes: (a) `hordeOpenWay`'s veto gains the valley field exactly as it already consults the player field ("the field says go round something: go round it"); (b) slot *selection* can prefer slots whose approach cell has finite, lower valley-dist — a slot behind a thicket wall stops being picked and immediately abandoned. Approach points themselves stay player-centred; the field owns getting there, the slots own arriving.

## 7. The tests that would prove it

Node-side first (the field code is pure; if Grokbot extracts it as `game/nav-field.js` the way story-chain/utilities were extracted, these run under `node --test` with a hand-made height/wall fixture, no game):

1. **The one gap:** a wall ring with one 2-cell gap; a zombie outside, HQ inside; simulated steps reach the HQ, and the path passes through the gap (assert min distance to the gap centre < 2 m; assert it never crosses a blocked cell). This is also the t-test form in the game (spawn, wake, walk, arrive within T seconds).
2. **Iron closes it:** same fixture with the gap ironed → no route (`dist === Infinity` round the ring) and the zombie does not arrive; the *player* still walks through (no solid added — assert `resolveStaticProps` unchanged there).
3. **A cut log opens one:** log solid blocking the gap → no route; cut event → topology delta → rebuilt field → route exists through the cut, and the rebuild was triggered by the event, not the timer (assert rebuild count).
4. **Costs:** on a fork (trail through woods vs open ground round), the field's chosen neighbour follows the trail (dist comparison), and a woods cell costs more than its ground neighbour (unit assert on the Dijkstra input).
5. **No regression:** the existing field tests (t80, t126's worker-adjacent behaviour, the flow suites) pass untouched; night-20 bench `--prof`: zombie tick and 1% low no worse than the pre-change seat (D-99) — with the worker on, the *only* main-thread addition is one `flowDirOn` read per chasing zombie per frame (two array reads and a normalise; the MU-9-style allocation audit should show zero new per-frame allocation).
6. **The stale-field case:** cut a log mid-wave; the zombie already en route re-routes within the worker round trip + a second (assert with the main-thread fallback too, where it's one rebuild later).
