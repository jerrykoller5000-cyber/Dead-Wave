# CL-134: the weapon sounds, overhauled (Claude, 2026-10-09)

Jerry: "Overhaul all weapons sounds. We are going for impactful and fun to hear. Players do a ton of shooting and
sound is so integral to gameplay." One task, solo.

**Listen first:** `review/weapon-sounds/index.html` (grey is before, gold is now, for every gun and the handling), or
`review/weapon-sounds/weapon-sounds-reel.mp3` (every gun before, then after; the timestamps are in its README).

## What was wrong (measured, not guessed)

Every sound was rendered offline through the real mix and measured (loudness, peak, spectrum, envelope):

- **Quiet and short.** A pistol round was -45 LUFS, quieter than a zombie dying (-41.5). Most shots were gone in a
  tenth of a second: no tail, no space, nothing for the valley to answer.
- **Beeps under the noise.** The body of most guns was a square or sawtooth wave sliding down in pitch: it read as
  a synth, not a gun. Little energy above 2.5 kHz, so no crack; most of it below 250 Hz, so on a laptop speaker
  almost nothing.
- **Suppressors changed nothing.** The can was bought and fitted but the shot sounded the same.
- **The handling was beeps too** (dry fire was a 120 Hz square, the reload cues triangles and squares), and each
  noise layer allocated a fresh noise buffer every time it played: costly under full auto.

## What it is now

- `core/gunsynth.js` builds every sound sample by sample (pure DSP, no Web Audio): a shot is a supersonic crack
  (an N-wave), a muzzle pop, a body (noise under a falling lowpass, with a resonant "bark" that is the gun's voice),
  a pitched thump and sub for the punch, and modal metal for the action (slide, bolt, hammer, links, the AR's buffer
  spring), driven in three groups so it is dense and loud but the front still cracks, then an EQ "voice" per gun.
  Every sound has 3 to 6 variants and plays with a little pitch and level jitter: no two rounds alike.
- **Tails.** Each gun has a stereo tail: a rumble that blooms and rolls, and the valley's echoes (dark, smeared
  copies of the shot at 0.1 to 2 s, wider left and right). The sniper's rolls for two and a half seconds; a burst's
  tails are turned down as the rounds stack, so a burst rolls instead of turning to mush.
- **Each gun its own.** Pistol: a tight snap and the slide. Wasp: a nasal "tak" and the open bolt clattering. M4: the
  crack, a compact punch, the buffer spring. AK: lower and chunkier, a heavier bolt. Watchman: a chesty chug and the
  links. Rotary gun: each game round is four, a 60th of a second apart, so it tears, over a motor that winds up and
  down with the barrels. Shotgun: a broadband front, a long low boom, the valley answering. Breacher-12: tighter, with
  its gas system. Revolver: the hammer falls, then a bright magnum. Sniper: the biggest crack in the game, doubled off
  the ground, a sub, two seconds of echo, then the bolt worked in the quiet. Launcher: a hollow "thoomp" and the round
  hissing away. Mortar: its own deeper tube (it used the launcher's). Explosions: a pressure front, a long low blast,
  three seconds of rolling tail and the dirt coming down.
- **Suppressed** (pistol, Wasp, shotgun, M4, AK, sniper): no crack to speak of, a dull "thup" through the baffles, the
  action suddenly loud by comparison, almost no tail, half the level.
- **Placed.** A pair of pistols fires left and right. Turrets, Brandt's Watchman and the roof rifles are heard from
  where they are (quieter, darker, more tail further off). Explosions too, and far ones arrive a little late.
- **Even bursts.** The game fires on frame boundaries; the audio places each round on the gun's beat (at most 25 ms
  late), and two rounds in one frame are spread instead of stacked.
- **The handling.** Dry fire is a hammer falling on nothing. The pump is two halves (back, then home). Reloads, the
  charging handle, the bolt, the AK's rock, the drum, the belt, the box, shells, brass ringing and bouncing, the
  mag hitting the ground: all modal metal and polymer now. Hits are a punch, a wet slap and a crunch; a headshot
  cracks. Blades whoosh and chop. The flamer catches with a whoomph.
- **The mix.** The guns are about 10 dB louder than before (the handling a few dB, and 8 to 10 under a shot), the bigger guns louder than the
  small ones (the targets are at the top of `tools/soundlab.mjs`). The glue compressor's attack went from 4 to 8 ms
  so the front of a shot gets through it, and a limiter at -1.5 dB after the master gain means nothing clips.
- **Cost.** Every bank is built off the main thread (`core/gunsynth-worker.js`) as soon as there is an audio context,
  about 20 MB of buffers in all. A round is now two buffer sources and two gains (it was up to a dozen nodes and new
  noise buffers every round). A sound wanted before its bank is in jumps the queue and plays a stand-in: the game
  never stalls for a sound (t220 saw 57 banks built in the worker, none on the main thread).
