# CL-186 · Claude · The marine's motion and look: the weight in his walk, his arms, and the shade on him

Jerry (2026-10-12): "a massive pass on Marine animation and his models fidelity. We need to nail his motion and look so
we do not look like all those other AI games ... All animations refined, All anatomy and textures improved. I
understand it is within reason. If it is unfeasible do not do it. Just look for every meaningful gain in your pass."

The baseline (the pose sheets and the gait measured in the game before any change): the studio clips give his legs,
hips, chest and head a good tempo; what was missing is what a figure is *read* by. His feet landed flat and left flat;
his pelvis never shifted over the planted foot; his hips and shoulders did not scissor; he started and stopped with
nothing in his body saying so; unarmed, his arms hung bowed (elbows out, the hands held in front of his belt as if
round a ball); and the rig was lit by the sun and the sky alone, so the armpits, the brow under the cap, the pack
against his back and the crotch were as bright as his shoulders: the flat look of a figure made of parts.

## 1. The weight in his walk and run — studio/marine-gait-feel.js (new, pure, node-tested)

Laid over the clips every frame by updateMarinePose, scaled by the clips' own weight (fades with the crouch), off
with `GAIT.weight = false`, every number live in `TT.gaitDbg.GAIT_WEIGHT`:

- **The foot roll.** The heel lands toe-up (0.18 rad walk, 0.12 run; kept small because the heel sinks by
  heelLen·sin of it), the sole is flat through mid-stance, the heel comes off ahead of the toe through the late
  stance — the pitch is the one that puts the ball of the foot back on the ground for the ankle's lift the clip gives
  it, capped at 0.5 — and the toe hangs through the swing by how free the clip's own `level` channel says the foot is
  (marineGait now passes `level` through; it was ignored). Measured: walk ankle pitch range −0.67..0.10 against the
  old flat −0.77..0.18 with the strike and push-off now in the right places (feet2/ sheets).
- **The pelvis over the planted foot.** ±2.1 cm walk, ±1.3 cm run, peaking 0.14 / 0.09 of a cycle after the strike;
  the thighs counter it (`legZ = −sway / legLen`) so the planted boot stays exactly where it was put.
- **The hips and shoulders scissor.** The pelvis turns 3.4° (walk) / 5.1° (run) with the stride, the forward leg's
  hip forward; the chest turns 60% back against it (on top of the clip's own chest twist), the thighs take half
  back so the boots keep near the travel line; the shoulder over the planted foot settles 1.4°. GB-149's twist cap
  and the hip-yaw solve were taught that this turn is the stride's, not a twist for the legs to chase.
- **The lean of his own weight.** The frame's change of velocity, in his own frame: 0.0022 rad per m/s², capped at
  0.12 rad (7°), eased at 7/s. A start leans him in, a stop leans him back (measured −0.06 rad through a stop from a
  run), a strafe's start leans him sideways, and a turn on the move banks him (the centripetal pull is the same
  number). With it a small dip (0.2 m per rad) and the head taking half back. On the ground only.
- No stride or cadence change: at 7.4 / 11.8 u/s on 0.52 m legs each step covers 2.45 / 2.65 u while a foot can
  reach 0.39 / 0.73, so the planted foot slides at 0.85 / 0.46 of the ground speed and nothing in the animation can
  fix that — it is the speeds (PHYS.walk / run) or the scale of the marine, Jerry's call. The weight above is what
  makes the gait read anyway.

Not done, and why: a pack on a spring (the pack is a flat bag against the back plate with the slung guns and holsters
fitted to it to the centimetre — CL-160 — and at R 12 a 1 cm bounce is half a pixel).

## 2. Unarmed arms (UNARMED_ARMS)

`{ x: -0.06, z: -0.11, elbow: -0.24 }` (was x −0.12, z −0.16, elbow −0.4): the upper arm hangs nearly straight, the
hand beside the thigh a touch forward of it, the elbow soft. The walk's and run's swing are unchanged (ab2/ sheets:
a0 old, a1 picked, a2 straighter, a3 between).

## 3. Baked shade, dust and wear — studio/marine-ao.js (new)

At build (makeMarine, after the merge) every mesh of his own wardrobe materials, skin and hardware gets a colour
attribute: the sky blocked by his own body, from 86 spheres on capsules between his joints with the arms hanging,
each owned by the joint group its part hangs from (a vertex is never shaded by its own part). With it: dust on the
boots (lightened toward dry earth, more toward the sole) and the trouser cuffs, and the fabric over the knees and
the points of the elbows rubbed lighter. `TT.aoDbg.bake({ strength, floor, spread, dust, wear })` re-bakes live;
defaults strength 1.0, floor 0.4, dust 0.5, wear 0.5. 78k vertices, 87 meshes, 90–190 ms in the headless
swiftshader box (so well under 100 ms on a real machine), once per marine built (the player, the dress preview, the
three survivors, the two of the burial crew). The shared hardware materials (pouches, patches, lenses, the carry
rig) are untouched, so nothing else in the world changes. The skin-merge shadow proxy reads vertex colours already.

## 4. Footfalls on the heel strikes (index.html, the movement block)

The footstep sound, the dust and the splash fire when the gait's phase crosses each foot's touchdown
(GAIT_WEIGHT.strike: walk 0.05 / run 0.036 of a cycle for the left, +0.5 for the right, backpedal reversed), and the
dust comes up under that boot (its ankle's world position), not under his root. They used to come every 2 m
(2.5 running) of travel, which drifted against the feet (a walk step is 2.45 u). Crouched, or with the clips off, the
distance trigger stays as it was.
