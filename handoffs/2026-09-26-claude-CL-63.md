# claude — CL-63 Scenes: the format, the marine rig, the scene player — 2026-09-26
Changed: Scenes (D-41), so the studio can see two bodies in one moment and the game can play the same file. `docs/studio.md` §9 is the `dw-scene/1` format: actors (a registered rig on a path, or at a fixed spot; `along`/`side` spacing, `face`, keyed `tilt` and `rise` for the whole body), their clips in time order with `fade`, `loop` and `stride` (metres per play of the clip: the clip then plays at the ground's speed, so planted feet stay planted), paths (a line plus keyed speed, integrated into distance), holds between bodies (`reach`: a limb goes to a joint on another body; `tow`: the held body is pulled along the ground so the grip stays at the hand; `lift`: the held limb goes up to the hand; `offset` along the limb), and checks. `studio/scene.js` plays it: `loadScene(json, clipOf)` names every problem, `createScene(scene, { parent, bodies })` → `update(dt)` returns events and per-frame checks, `seek(t)` is deterministic for the renderer, `worst` keeps the worst of each check. Checks: hold gap (at full reach/tow), slide (a limb on the ground moving over it since touchdown; "on the ground" is either planted by the clip or down at its rest standing height, so a clip that plants nothing is still caught gliding; towed bodies and holding limbs skipped), ground speed per actor, one-frame snap. Holds order the bodies (held before holder), loops are refused. Bodies can be the host's own: they are placed in their own parent's frame, so the game's marine can stay under the player's roll pivot. The marine is a rig now (`studio/marine.js`): a stand-in built on makeMarine()'s exact joint offsets, or `create({ group })` adopts the game's marine by its userData names; its limbs aim the real end point (`ikLimb(..., endLocal)`, the grip sits ahead of the forearm). Player: `applyPose(..., { reach })`, `solveChain`. A quick look: `studio/scene-preview.html?scene=demo-drag` (side-on tiles following the bodies, red with the check's name where one fails). A demo scene `studio/scenes/demo-drag.json` and a stand-in marine clip `studio/clips/marine/lie.json` for CU-46 to render until CL-64's.
What the demo already shows (v1 guardian clips, not the finished grab): the hand lands on and holds the marine's ankle and he is towed on his back; the checks flag the v1 drag's feet sliding up to 2.2 m while down (the gliding Jerry saw), a 0.84 m miss when the hand first reaches (the marine starts out of reach), and a ~0.13 m gap while hauling because the hand holds higher than his lifted leg can reach.
