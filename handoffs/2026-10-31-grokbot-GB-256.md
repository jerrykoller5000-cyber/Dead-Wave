# GB-256: His hands in third person: the right-click shake and the bouncing guns (J31, J32)

Grokbot, 2026-10-31. index.html SHA-256 418412EB544BCE06BC4FE7228A086FA0FC35A83B3C3124475A5E4AD06777843F (one line changed). Backup of the start: qa/2026-10-31-GB-256/index.before.html (D8137A4F..., the GB-251 page). No git.

## The cause
Not the kick hold, the re-hold or the gun lag: it is the aim. In the Training Ground the cursor's ray hits the floor twice in updateMouseAimBody: once as the ground (raycastTerrain, which in training is trainingFloorHit) and once as a range hit (traceTraining also returns the floor, solid.floor). The two distances are the same floor point in two units (world metres vs a fraction of TRAINING.FAR), so `trainingHit.t * TRAINING.FAR <= groundT` was a tie that floating point broke differently every frame. aimKind flipped between 'ground' and 'training', and shotAimTarget raises only a 'ground' aim to chest height (AIM_CHEST). So every other frame or so the gun pitched about 6 deg down at the floor and back: the grip and the mount jumped 25-30 mm up and down. Holding right-click (the 2x aim zoom) magnifies it on screen; moving the cursor sets it off. Open ground in a match never has a range hit, which is why it is the Training Ground.

## The change
index.html line 48284: the range trace's floor hit no longer competes with the floor's own hit (`!trainingHit.solid.floor`). The floor now always reads 'ground', the same as open ground in a match (chest-height aim over the cursor). Plates, pillars, walls and glass still win when they are in front, as before.
