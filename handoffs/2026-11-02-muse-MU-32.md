## 0. Shared facts (read once)

- **The clearing a place actually gets:** the woods scatter keeps trees 14 m off every POI (`nearPOI(x, z, 14)`, index.html:18757; CU-206 dense woods elsewhere), CU-216 rings each clearing with that place's tree kinds just outside it (index.html:18696), and foliage clears inside camps/yards at build. So every place below has an effective clearing radius of **≈14 m** (the HQ's is 24, the landing's 9). A ring at clearing + 4 m sits at **r = 18 m, length 2π×18 ≈ 113 m** of wall band per place — seven places ≈ 790 m of band, built by the existing `makeWallBand` machinery (MU-22; HQ ring r60 + quarter bands are live, index.html:17541ff, with gaps computed from PATHS crossings by `ringTrailGaps`/`bandTrailGaps`).
- **Walking round through the trees: yes, for him — the trees don't block, only trunks do** (trunk solids; gaps between trunks are everywhere at any density). A *ring* is precisely the thing that changes that answer; without the ring, every "gate" today is a point block on one trail that a player can stroll around through the woods. With the ring, the only ways in are its gaps.
- **Chain gates today are point gates** (`chainGates`, index.html:33782; on unless ?chaingates=0): each sits on the main approach (chainSay texts at index.html:30698 depot, :31017 mast, :31828 ranger, :32199 trapper; toolbox-chain is a padlock, :31614) and blocks *interactions* past it (MU-12/MU-30) — it does not fence the place.
- **The dead at night:** the valley field marks every wall band's solids as blocked (index.html:56668) and routes the dead through band gaps (GP-20006 note, index.html:17501) — a closed ring with a *locked gate in its only gap* blocks the dead too while locked (the gate is a solid), and lets them in when he opens it. That symmetry is the design question per place (§8), not an accident.
- **Guards inside before the day:** POI guards spawn from day 3 at their camps regardless of the lead's day (MU-14: `poiGuardsDue`, asleep at posts, woken by reach/hit/alarm) — a closed ring built from day 1 would pen them inside with no way out until he opens the gate (they'd wake and press the ring from inside; funny once, broken as a system). Any ring must spawn outside, wake-and-wait at the gate, or the ring's day must follow the guards'. **Recommendation for all rows: guards' posts move outside the ring line or onto the gate gap.**

## 8. The three hardest rings

1. **The medical convoy (and its sibling the truck).** They aren't yards — they're wrecks on through-roads, rolled to a seed-chosen bend. A 113 m ring fences empty trees, can cut a valley-field corridor the dead use, and gates a site whose lock is already the jack/cooler (convoy) or the toolbox padlock (truck). Hardest because the ring answers a question these places don't ask: their blocker should stay *on the road* (a barrier across the bend, MU-12 §2.3) with the trees round them left open — which also means he can walk round it… and for the convoy that's arguably right: the cooler is the lock, the barrier is the *announcement*. If CL-296 wants rings anyway, these two need the road's far-side gap preserved and the ring centred on the *bend*, not the wreck.
2. **The trapper's camp.** Already a fenced yard with a gate, behind a river he can wade. A second ring is pure duplication *unless* its job is different: penning the *approach* (the clearing between river and fence) so the first sight of the yard is at the ring's gate. The river side can never be closed by a land band (water gaps), so "closed" here is a fiction — design it as a *front* (gate + partial band tying into the river banks), not a ring.
3. **The mast yard.** The mission is two places (cabinet clearing + a vault 9.5–27.5 m away, re-placed per run). One ring either excludes the vault (the mission leaks: he could reach the vault's outside without the cabinet — though its keypad still locks it) or swells to r ≈ 32 (200 m of band, the longest of the seven, round a *tower*). The honest shape is probably two small enclosures or a ring + a walled *path* between them (bands can be lines, not only rings — the quarter bands are lines).

**And the general note for CL-296:** five of seven places are spurs (dead ends), where a ring + one gate is clean, cheap (113 m each) and safe for the dead's routes; the two wrecks are the exceptions, and the trapper is a front, not a ring. The guards-inside problem (§0) applies to all three camps and must be answered once, globally, before any ring is switched on.
