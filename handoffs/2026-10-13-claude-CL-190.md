# CL-190 · Claude · The AI disclosure before the studio intro

Jerry (2026-10-13): "We are creating a disclaimer before the Game starts ... We want to be 100% transparent about
using AI from the rip. I like using AI but some people may not." His spec, line by line, is what plays.

## What plays (assets/intro/disclaimer.js + disclaimer.css, assets/disclaimer/)

1. Completely black. Soft strobing red (left) and blue (right) police lights, with a thin lightbar at the top.
2. **Police Siren.mp3** under hard red/blue flashes (2.2 Hz a side, 2.1 s).
3. The instant it ends, **Been Warned.mp3** starts and the lights go soft; the song is ducked under each voice.
4. Each line floats in (up from below, out of a blur), its voice starts 0.3 s later, it holds 0.6 s after the
   voice, floats out, 0.35 s gap:
   - **ATTENTION!** in red (Black Ops One, a punch-in, the lights flare once) · `1 .Attention!.mp3`
   - *This game was made entirely using artificial intelligence.* · `2.This_Game.mp3` · B1 chirps halfway through
     (cue 1.58 s; B1 has 0.27 s of silence in front, so the chirp lands at 1.85 of the 3.71 s line)
   - *Whether you like it...or it's just more soulless AI slop...is up to you.* · `3. Whether you like it.mp3` ·
     B1 in the pause between "slop" and "is" (the pause is 3.50–3.85 s into the line; cue 3.21)
   - *But I do hope you are able to have some fun.* · `4. Have Fun.mp3`
   - *Now go on! Git!* · `5. Git!.mp3` · 0.1 s after "Git!" the rifle fires (`z-sounds/weapons/rifle/rifle-1.ogg`):
     the text fades on the shot, a short muzzle flash, the lights go out, the song is cut, 0.75 s of black, then
     the fade to the caracal.
   The chirping guy is the second voice: low-passed (1.5 kHz), half-left, through a feedback echo (210 ms, 45 %,
   darker still), at a fifth of the main voice — "in the distance with echo so you can barely hear it".
5. **Skip**: Esc, Enter, Space, or the Skip button (bottom right). The key or click that starts it (below) is
   never taken as a skip.

Without the dancer: 2.1 s siren + 22.3 s of song ≈ 25 s; with him (below) ≈ 71 s. Levels follow the player's SFX
volume (tt_vol_sfx).

## Sound needs the browser's say-so

Browsers block sound before a gesture. If the context starts suspended, the lights strobe over
"PRESS ANY KEY OR TAP TO BEGIN" and the first key/click starts the show. `Play Dead-Wave.bat` now launches Edge
with `--autoplay-policy=no-user-gesture-required`, so from the launcher it starts by itself (and the caracal plays
with sound after it, no "Play intro with sound" button). Jerry: if that flag ever bothers you, drop it from the
bat and the prompt comes back — nothing else changes.

## Built to survive the load

The game module parses and builds the world behind the black screen while this plays, and that holds the main
thread for seconds at a stretch (measured headless: a first cue from script landed 2.5 s late). So nothing that is
heard or seen is cued from script once it starts: every clip, duck and the shot are scheduled on the audio clock up
front (the song is decoded whole — 55 MB for its 2:24 — and dropped when the sequence ends), and every light
pattern, line (in and out), the flare, the flash and the final fade are CSS animations committed in one go with
their cue times as delays, pinned to the audio clock's instant (`Animation.startTime`), so the compositor keeps
them in time while the main thread is busy. Only the window events and the hand-off to the opening wait for it.

## The opening (assets/intro/opening.js, index.html)

- `#openingVideo` lost its `autoplay` attribute; opening.js starts it when `DWDisclaimer.done` resolves (under the
  disclosure's fade-out), `preload="auto"` buffers it meanwhile.
- The watchdog and `intro()` (a video that errors or ends) wait while the disclosure is active, so a missing
  caracal still shows the title after the disclosure, not during it.
- `DWOpening.dismissForTesting()` dismisses the disclosure first (instant, silent), so the harnesses and
  tools/tests/run-all.mjs behave as before. `?disclaimer=0` on the URL skips it for a session.
- Asset versions: `opening.js?v=cl190`, `disclaimer.css/js?v=cl190`.

## GPT's dancing marine is in (GP-19902)

GPT finished the minute while this was being built (review/dance-GP19902/, handoffs/2026-10-13-chatgpt-GP-19902.md):
his transparent render is now assets/disclaimer/marine-dance.webm (VP9 with alpha, cropped to the dancer — 612×560
of the 1280×720 frame, 12.5 MB instead of 24), a `<video>` on center stage. It is kept on the song's clock: it
starts at song second 9.15 (the clip's first beat, GPT's `sourceStart`), fades in there, small drift is taken up by
the playback rate, a stall by one seek (never a storm of them), and it pauses on its last frame at 60 s; the
lines move to the bottom while he is there and spread over his minute (the `dance` cue preset: song 0.4, 3.6,
14.2, 31.0, 67.45; the shot at ~69.3 lands on his last hit, the clip ending at 69.15). With him in, the whole
thing runs ~71 s (siren + 69.3 s of song + the tail); Esc skips it. A faint spot on the floor stands in for the
shadow the alpha pass lost. The stage frame is lifted and scaled (`#disclaimerStage.frame` transform:
translate(-50%, -57%) scale(.9)) so his floor moves clear the text; both are a line of CSS to taste.

Knobs: `DWDisclaimer.cfg.dancer` (`on: false` → no dancer, tight cues, ~25 s; `start`, `crop`, `drift`, `seek`),
`cfg.preset` ('auto' picks the dance cues when he is on the stage as the run starts; 'tight' / 'dance' force).
`DWDisclaimer.attach(el)` replaces him with anything else; `songTime()`, `timeline`, `bpm`, `dancer` and the
`dw-disclaimer-*` events are there for whoever drives something live. GPT's live path (ui/disclaimer-dance.js on a
fresh makeMarine()) is not used: the render keeps time through the load stalls, a script-driven canvas would not.
