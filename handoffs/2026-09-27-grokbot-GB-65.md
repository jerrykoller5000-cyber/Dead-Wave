# GB-65 (R1 · P-70, P-6): the dead react in the game

Changed:
- Each zombie is adopted as the studio's `zombie` rig the first time it is hit, and gets a reacting body (studio/motion.js) with its kind's preset: brutes and soldiers `zombie/brute`, ferals and leapers `zombie/feral`, everything else `zombie/shambler`. At most 8 bodies simulate at once (`createMotionPool({ max: 8 })`).
- Rounds, pellets, blades, the chainsaw, blasts and crushes call `body.hit`. A rifle round jolts a zombie, a close shell puts a shambler down, and a grenade throws it. It gets up again.
- A hit the body can't take plays today's flinch, stagger and knockdown instead. That covers a full pool, a kind with no body (spiders, the colossus, the guardian), and a zombie that is crawling, climbing a window, rising or leaping.
- While a body is falling, down or getting up, the AI does nothing of its own. It doesn't attack, trample, seek windows or climb. The body's drift moves the zombie, and hard builds still stop it.
- P-6: the pellets of one pull share a shot id. Each zombie reacts once to their sum, whether that's a body hit or today's stagger and knockdown (which never fired for a shell before, because each pellet was too small). A shell pays only one headshot bonus. A 40+ damage shell into a zombie within 4 m gives a small camera kick.
- The body's push comes from the round's damage before armour. Armour soaks the wound, not the shove, and the preset's mass decides who stays up. Without this, a demon (armour 0.18) fell just short of going down.
- killZombie gives the body's slot back and puts the animated pose back first, because zombie meshes are pooled.

## How

**Adoption.**
- `zombieBody(z)` adopts lazily on the first hit: `Studio.rigs.get('zombie').create({ group: z.mesh })`, then `createBody(inst, preset, { ground, pool })`.
- The ground is `entityGroundY` at the zombie's own height plus a step, so a zombie on a deck falls onto the deck.
- If adoption throws, the zombie keeps `body = null` and plays the old reaction for life. The console warns once.
- `rigs.js createInstance` builds a whole reference rig on every adoption (to copy the studio's rest). That is an allocation on each zombie's first hit, which is why I sent the request to Claude.

**A hit.**
- `bodyHit` skips a zombie that is crawling, climbing a window, rising or leaping (counted as `skipped`).
- A sleeping body reads its animated pose twice before the hit (`follow()`), so it wakes where it stands at rest.
- The push in m/s, from the damage before armour:
  - an AK round (27) about 2.5;
  - a whole shell up close (63) about 6.5;
  - a blast 2 + 0.06 × damage, capped at 12;
  - a blade 0.12 × damage;
  - a crush 0.05 × damage, capped at 10.
- If the body or the pool refuses, `reactToHit` plays today's reaction on the damage after armour, as before (counted as `refused`).
- Lost limbs are only hidden (`detachZombiePart`), so a body still works without them. A crawler lies low on purpose, so it has no body.

**Shells (P-6).**
- The pellet loop in `fire` sets `curShotId`, and `spawnProjectile` stores it on each pellet.
- `damageZombie` still applies each pellet's damage, blood and dismemberment at once. It only defers the reaction: it sums `_shotN`, `_shotDmg`, `_shotRaw`, the hit height and the direction per zombie.
- `flushShotHits()` runs right after `updateProjectiles` and reacts once per zombie.
- The headshot bonus is paid once per shot id.

**The AI waits.**
- `motionDown(z)` is true while the body is `fall`, `down` or `getup`.
- `updateZombies` treats that like `knockT > 0`. On the `fall` event the attack windup is cleared, so a zombie doesn't finish a swing from the floor.
- The body pass (`updateMotionBodies`) runs after the zombies posed their animation, and before the window-climb loop: follow, update, apply. It moves the zombie by `drift` through `resolveHardBuildCollisions`, and lets a body go when it sleeps, dies, starts crawling or leaves the zombie list.

**Cost (headless, fakethree).**
- With 48 zombies and 8 bodies reacting, the pass measured 8.7 ms a frame (EMA).
- Per body per frame that is about 0.12 ms for follow, 0.65 ms for update and 0.40 ms for apply. All three are studio/motion.js; my code around them is a loop and one collision call.
- Claude's own motion test reports 2.6 ms for 48 bodies in Node, so most of this is probably fakethree's math.
- t90's bar is one 60 Hz frame (16 ms) headless, written as such in the test. The "hold the frame" number that matters is Jerry's GPU (devBench, P-77), which I couldn't measure.

**t90 checks:**
- A close shell: 7 pellets become one hit (63 damage), and the body takes it once. The shambler goes down, lies there with the AI counting it as down and no windup, then gets up.
- An AK round: the body reacts and stays up, and is still up 150 ms later.
- A grenade throws a shambler: it is down and moved at least 0.3 m.
- On their bodies, a close shell drops a demon but not a brute or a soldier. A spider has no body and doesn't go down.
- The old reaction (bodies off, as when the pool is full), on the summed shell:
  - a shell at 1.5 m gives at least 3.5 m/s and `knockT > 0`, more than an AK round, which leaves it standing;
  - the demon goes down; the brute, the soldier and the spider don't.
  - This is the P-6 acceptance as written.
- Seven pellets in the head pay one headshot bonus.
- 48 zombies and ten close shells: 8 bodies take the hit, 2 are refused by the full pool and play the old knockdown, and none are skipped. The body pass fits in a frame headless. Cleared zombies give their pool slots back.
- No page errors.
- While pellets land, the test pins `Math.random` so nothing comes off. A zombie that loses both legs crawls (and gets no body), and a lost head leaves it on 1 hp. Either made the checks a coin toss.
