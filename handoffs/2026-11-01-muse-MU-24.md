## 1. What exists today

- **The flood:** one `PointLight(0xffb858, 0, 15, 1.8)` at the house (index.html:26769 — **range 15 m, decay 1.8**), turned to 2.4 at night only when powered, not blacked out, and past a sky-darkness threshold (index.html:32136); the same branch requests two pooled effect lights (range 12) at the yard corners. **Bay lamps:** `HQ_BAY_LAMP_I = 1.85`, flicker with the power stutter (`hqPowerFlicker`, 1.4 s on restoration, index.html:32117), 0.04 unpowered; panel/battery dimming on blackout (MU-15). All of this is inside the **light pool** (CL-116: `createLightPool`, size 4; real PointLights are adopted/pooled by distance — the game has a hard small budget of true lights, which is exactly why nothing lights the sky).
- **The mast's beacon** is the one distant-light precedent in the world: a blinking red beacon at the mast top (index.html:6396, kept as a campfire entry with `beacon: true`, its own hours at index.html:10254), and GP-20003's skyline added its long-range read: a **Sprite with `fog: false`, manual distance fade** ("Night fog ends at 65 m, before the 150 m mast; fade it by distance ourselves", game/skyline-pulls.js:125–126). That sprite is the pattern §3 copies.

## 2. Why no glow is seen today

Three independent reasons, any one sufficient:

1. **The light physically reaches 15 m.** A PointLight with distance 15/decay 1.8 contributes nothing at the canopy 50–150 m away, and there is no emissive sky/haze object over the HQ. The yard is lit; the sky above it is the unlit sky dome.
2. **Fog eats the distance anyway.** At dusk the linear fog runs near 18 → far 65 m + twilight scaling (index.html:2825: `near = 18 + twilight×172`, `far = 65 + twilight×575` — mid-twilight ≈ 40/350, deep dusk closing to ~18/65); clear night far is 160 m (index.html:2833), and the wave's night-mist clamps far to **32 m** (`FOG_NIGHT`, index.html:2897). Anything *in the world* at HQ distance from a player caught out in the woods is fogged to the fog colour — lit or not. This is by design (it hides the far woods, CU-208) and it is also why the mast beacon needed a fog-exempt sprite.
3. **No one asks for it:** `updateHQ` drives lamps/flood/fans for the yard; the skyline pulls (GP-20003) glow the *mast*, not home. There is no HQ-glow object to turn on.

## 3. The cheapest ways to show one, in order

**A. A sky-glow sprite over the HQ clearing (recommended; ~1 draw).** Copy the mast beacon's construction exactly (game/skyline-pulls.js:126): a `SpriteMaterial` with the radial glow texture, `fog: false`, `depthWrite: false`, additive-ish warm colour matched to the flood (0xffb858), scaled large (a glow, not a bulb: tens of metres across, centred ~15–25 m over the HQ so it crowns the canopy from most angles), with **manual distance fade**: opacity rising as he gets farther (invisible in the yard — he can see the real lamps; this is a *wayfinding* light), peaking in the 60–200 m band, fading beyond ~250 m so it never reads across the whole map. Power rule: visible exactly when the flood is on — read the same condition as index.html:32136 (`powered && night-ish && !blackout`, plus lockdown-red recolour comes free if it reads the flood's colour). Flicker: inherit `hqPowerFlicker` (the 1.4 s stutter when power returns would play in the sky — a lovely confirmation from a valley away, and free).
- *Fog behaviour:* exempt (`fog: false`) like the beacon; the manual fade replaces fog's job. Occlusion: sprites don't depth-test against canopy unless depthTest is on — keep `depthTest: true` so trees in front still silhouette (the glow *over* the canopy should be blocked by a near trunk, not drawn through the wood he's inside — actually depthTest true gives exactly "seen over/through gaps", which is the desired read; AG to judge).
- *Draw cost:* one sprite = one draw, no light, no shadow. It can reuse the beacon's radial texture. This is the whole feature at minimum size.

**B. A lit haze dome over the clearing (prettier, ~1 draw, more tuning).** An inverted sphere/cone of additive gradient over the HQ (the fogMistDome construction, index.html:2919, is the in-repo dome precedent: BackSide, transparent, depthWrite false, fog false), opacity low, coloured as A. Reads as light *in the air* (mist catching the flood) and scales with the yard, but risks looking like a force field at wrong opacities and needs sky-occlusion care at its rim. Only if A reads too flat on AG's shots.

**C. Real light thrown at the canopy (rejected):** raising the flood's range or adding a SpotLight up would need the light pool's scarce slots, lights only the nearest trees' undersides, and still dies in fog (reason 2). The beacon-sprite family exists precisely because real lights can't do distance in this game.

**Also consider (cheap, complements A):** the flood's *pool* of light already brightens the yard's ground disc; from a ridge, that lit ground + A's glow is the full "home" read. And the way home needs the *negative* too: on a no-power night (day 2 until he pours), home is *dark* — which is the story working (the utilities spec: he navigates by the mast beacon and memory until power returns). The glow must obey `hqPowered` strictly, or it spoils the run's darkest night.

## 4. Touchpoints for a build

One sprite + its update in `updateHQ`'s flood branch (index.html:32136: same on/off condition, flicker and colour), opacity by distance to the player each frame (one hypot — the branch already runs per frame; no new loop), `?hqglow=0` switch per CL-288 house style, TT exposure (`hqGlowDbg` opacity/visible) so tests can assert "glow on iff powered && night" without a screenshot, AG shots from two wooded bearings at dusk and at the wave's mist (32 m fog — the sprite must survive even that, since dusk-caught-out is the case; it does, being fog-exempt, but the *canopy silhouettes* against it will be mist-grey: check the read is still "home", not "smudge").
