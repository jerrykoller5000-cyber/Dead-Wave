## How

**The measurement.** At the end of makeZombieMesh (before `baseHipsY` is taken):
- `g.updateMatrixWorld(true)`.
- Walk the vertices of every mesh under each leg's `shinG` (the shin and the boot) and take the lowest world y.
- If it's below 0, raise the hips by it.
- g sits at the origin at that point, so world is the zombie's own frame.
- This is plain array math on `geometry.attributes.position`, so it works the same on three and on fakethree.
- It runs once per built mesh. Pooled meshes keep theirs.

**hitH.** After the lift:
- Measure the head subtree's highest vertex and scale it by `vr.height`, since spawnZombie stretches the group by it and hitH doesn't follow.
- `hitH = max(table, top·vr.height + 0.08·s)`.
- 0.04·s wasn't enough: a spawn pose put some crowns 1–3 cm past it.
- 0.12·s put the spitter's and screamer's head centres under the 0.78 headshot line. 0.08·s satisfies both.

**Reacting bodies.** studio/motion.js measures the sink from the animated pose when a body wakes. That was about −0.25 before and is about 0 now. t93 checks the feet during a stagger: deepest −0.03 m, and −0.03 after.

**t91 (1).** I re-based the rifle shot from +1.0 to +1.0 + footLift, because +1.0 alone now hits the pelvis; `__dbgKill` confirmed the kill lands at chest height. Even so, the limp body sits back about two runs in three: the hips go about 0.6 m along the shot and the head lags. With the lift switched off it was 3/3.

My reading: with the old sink, a body going limp had its feet's ground rise from −0.25 to 0 over 0.5 s (`sinkNow → 0` outside react and getup). That kicked the legs up and tipped the upper body forward along the shot. With the feet on the ground, nothing does that, and a weak rifle kill folds either way. That's the kill/limp preset: GB-70 (Jerry's lab notes), or Claude's call. I haven't touched presets. Checked out with --review.

**Brute.** Its table hitH is well over its head, so lowering it would shrink its target, which is gameplay. It's left alone and flagged.
