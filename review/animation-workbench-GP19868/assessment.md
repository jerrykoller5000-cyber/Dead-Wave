# Animation workbench assessment — GP-19868

Recommendation: **add diagnostics only**. The proposal's diagnosis is plausible, but most of its clip, reference, preview and review infrastructure already exists. The useful missing piece is a reliable, compact account of the **final runtime pose and its gameplay context**. There is no measured evidence yet that a separate editing service would outperform a better use of the current tools. No implementation was performed.

Reviewed both supplied proposal formats; the Markdown is used for section references. Document suggestions are hypotheses, not instructions to build. ## What the repository actually does

The engine is vendored Three.js r175 (`vendor/three/three.core.js:6`), native ES modules with no package dependencies/build step (`package.json`). The game uses WebGPURenderer with WebGL fallback (`index.html:1744`). This is not an imported FBX/GLTF skeleton driven throughout by AnimationMixer.

`makeZombieMesh` constructs a hierarchy of Groups: hips → torso → head/upper arms → elbows; hips → thighs → shins. Type-specific concept geometry replaces the original body surfaces (`index.html:32058`). Boots may be separated into additional ankle pivots before compaction and skin binding (`32118`). `skinZombieMesh` batches surfaces into SkinnedMesh objects, using existing parent Groups as skeleton entries and assigning each vertex one rigid weight (`32278–32363`). Rendering is skinned, but animation authoring is still mostly direct articulated transforms. Hidden rigid source surfaces remain available for hit testing and dismemberment. Bone scale collapse, far LOD and pool reset also affect the displayed result.

There are three different authoring paths:

- Studio JSON clips (`dw-clip/1`), two-bone IK, crossfade, planting and optional root motion (`studio/clip.js:236,382`). Guardian scene assets and marine walk/run reuse this system. Scene playback supports fixed-step replay and grip/slide/speed/snap diagnostics (`studio/scene.js:764,838`).
- Special marine hand paths (`dw-hand-path/1`), plus weapon-hold/recoil/idle/turn logic. Marine gait samples and blends walk/run clips, then analytically converts foot targets into thigh/knee angles (`index.html:17665`); the rest of the gameplay pose and arm layers follow (`50737–50765`).
- Recent zombie movement/attack helpers in `game/*`: Shambler foot retarget and inertial steering, Feral accents/claws/pose echoes, Spider-specific limbs and mouth/corpse poses, Drowned/Military/Brute state-dependent poses. These are JavaScript formulas and state machines, not generally editable keyframe tracks (`game/shambler-motion.js:60`, `game/feral-motion.js:36`, `game/brute-behavior.js:20,59`).

The zombie frame restores prior overlays and resolves action/LOD eligibility (`index.html:47414`), chooses pursuit/attack steering, resolves movement, assigns root heading (`48244`), updates gait phase (`48253`), writes generic limbs/secondary motion and action reactions, then applies type-specific overlays (`48699–48712`). **Separation, contact corrections and ground reseating happen afterward** (`48790–48853`). Main tick then applies enemy abilities, Feral pose echoes and separate Guardian boss poses (`50778–50783`), and later projectiles/hit effects. A reliable diagnostic must sample the fully composed frame with matrices current, after these writers; a source clip sample or a guessed callback time is insufficient. The normal game uses elapsed-clock dt capped at .05 seconds (`50419`), not fixed simulation stepping.

`studio/zombie.js:33` builds a box stand-in; `adoptZombie:96` maps an actual body, but creates empty end markers using common offsets. Adoption is therefore not itself a non-mutating inspection operation. A read-only report should inspect existing userData references directly. This is not a calibrated universal adapter: current ankle pivots, per-type internal scale, Feral palm axes, Spider limbs and armor bones require explicit profiles. Applying a Studio pose resets every tracked joint to rest first (`studio/clip.js:240`), so injecting a clip without a layer boundary could erase approved weapon/attack poses.

## Why prompting repeats

These are ranked explanations supported by source and recent corrections, not a controlled causal benchmark.

1. **Observation and coordinate interpretation.** Feral feedback progressed from more flail/bend to downward palms, separate slashes and stronger weight transfer. GP-19850 records an initial upward curl caught visually. The final helper compensates the entire root/hips/torso/shoulder chain and explicitly records sculpted local +Z palm/curl and -Y finger axes (`game/feral-motion.js:55–61`). Final actual-body checks measured palm world-Y ≤-.958 and minimum lateral clearance .434 m (`review/feral-claws-GP19850/live-check.mjs:18` and its proof). A semantic axes/profile report and transition strip would reduce this rediscovery; changing a local Euler number alone would not.

2. **Gameplay motion and layer ordering.** The original direct yaw assignment can turn instantly; a smoother clip cannot change pursuit trajectory. Shambler's fix limits angular rate/acceleration and locks attack direction (`game/shambler-steering.js:3,19`). Its curved pursuit exposed a 4.253 mm wall overlap after a later contact correction, requiring a collision backstop, not another animation key (`handoffs/2026-10-11-chatgpt-GP-19854.md:14`). Root movement after pose evaluation and ability overrides need context and preservation checks. A workbench could localize them; fixing them remains controller/combat work.

3. **Creative direction develops through viewing.** Longer ghost trails, panther-like slashes, heavier shuffles and more aggressive grabs are meaningful refinements, not necessarily unsuccessful implementations of a frozen specification. GP-19849 → GP-19850 is clear evidence. Better references/briefs might reduce ambiguity, but no solver can infer an unexpressed preference. Report defect corrections separately from newly requested creative changes.

4. **Representation/calibration gaps.** More expressive feet required real ankle pivots before skin binding. Brute armor work initially targeted surfaces that the concept pass subsequently replaced (GP-19867 handoff). A UI that edits a generic skeleton would conceal these project-specific constraints. Native source geometry and semantic mappings help; universal retargeting does not remove calibration work.

5. **Fragmented evidence and tool friction.** Recent tasks independently implement browser setup, MediaRecorder captures, pose sampling, thresholds and baseline snapshots. They use wall-clock waits, random variants/attack sides and per-task camera choices, rather than a common synchronized manifest. The camera can stay warm/reload, but typical native fixture boot cost was ~16–19 seconds; its source notes a ~14-second first-shelf warmup. Raw artifacts do not consistently carry all code/rig/config hashes or intent/contact provenance. Existing tests sometimes measure proxies rather than the actual visible surface: t222 classifies a simulated cylinder-height slice (`tools/tests/t222.js:67–72`); GP-19867 retained the same head-proxy failure before 31/36 and after 32/36, alongside separate actual posed-head ray checks. Better evidence can prevent chasing the wrong failure.

No measured model-capability comparison, human-time ledger or correction-turn benchmark was found. These findings do not establish a model intelligence ceiling, nor a predicted savings percentage.

## Proposal fit and reuse

The useful pieces are §2A's native-state adapter, §2C's focused context and versioning, and §2E/§4's runtime evaluation. Reuse the existing semantic rig naming where verified; extend it with one actual-body profile. Preserve existing rules for exclusive file ownership/fresh reads and immutable review baselines. Do not create a generic accept/revert command that restores whole index snapshots: it could overwrite another crew member's work.

The reference library in §2B largely exists: 23 locally catalogued motions, 23 bones, 30 fps in `assets/anim/reference/ual.json`, a catalogue, and a repository license record declaring Quaternius CC0. `studio/reference.js` provides inspection/mannequin playback; `studio/retarget-ual.mjs` already scales marine hip-to-ankle vectors and flips reference facing. Reuse native approved marine gait and prior approved zombie motion as timing/style guidance. Repository provenance was inspected; original pack/license history was not externally re-audited. Humanoid motions are not plug-and-play Spider or Guardian clips. No new assets, embeddings, cloud service or MCP server are justified.

§2D's editing service is premature. Some controls are readable constants (Brute/steering configuration), but others are embedded formulas, guarded overlays or private WeakMap state. There is no universal bounded-edit API. New sliders or apply_clip operations would require a validated ownership/override boundary and rollback semantics; existing clip validation and narrow fresh-file edits already handle the simpler cases. Do not convert every zombie into clips merely to fit the proposal.

Existing alternatives are substantive: `tools/studio.mjs` produces versioned clip/strip/video/stats; `studio/scene.js` supplies deterministic scene seek and measurements; `tools/camera.mjs` supplies a persistent actual-game browser, eval, camera reuse and screenshots; `tools/camera-rig.js:183` removes shelf zombies from the live update list, whereas `live:<type>` selects actual actors. Use the latter for behavior. Motion Lab supplies replay, muscle sliders and notes for stand-in reactions, not full live chase/action evaluation. The crew review/notes pipeline already supplies approval/history.

Current commands expose maintenance debt: `node tools/studio.mjs list` exits 1 because it treats marine knife-slash's hand-path JSON as a dw-clip/1. Focused Node checks report 33 pass/1 fail, with guardian cost 41 draws/19,582 triangles against a 160/8,000 budget. They also report existing guardian clip snaps (gallop 1.20 rad, pull 1.00 rad per frame). These are real current tool/source results, not proof those exact source defects reach the displayed Guardian in all gameplay states. No repairs were made. A new front end should not be built over these assumptions without capability checks.

## Smallest useful intervention

One **opt-in runtime evidence report**, around existing capture tools; no editor or editing service. First profile: **Shambler**, because approved marine-derived feet, smooth steering and missed-lunge recovery already provide useful reference behavior. Start with a flat Training-room scenario: straight chase → player crosses behind → committed miss → recovery. Add reaction interruption and pool reset as protected checks, not creative tuning tasks.

Record actual root displacement/yaw, local and world joint transforms, gait phase, action/LOD/lost-limb flags, ground height, simulation timestamps and dt. Use the real ankle/boot geometry instead of assumed Studio end markers. Produce gameplay and diagnostic views with timestamped transition strips plus a short summary. Hash source code, rig/profile, clip/config, camera and scenario inputs; mark old artifacts stale rather than silently reuse them.

First diagnostics: angular velocity/acceleration and root-versus-hip lag around a reversal; sole ground clearance and support-foot displacement around turns. Support contact is **inferred** unless authored or explicitly logged. Measure relative to the support surface; exclude deliberate heel/ball pivot, miss stumble, hopping, water and reactions. Generic snap scores would wrongly penalize the approved Feral's intentional tics. Existing .3-rad/5-cm Studio defaults are not universal acceptance thresholds.

Integration belongs at an opt-in post-composition/post-matrix sampling point owned by Cursor, with combat state/profile definitions reviewed by Grokbot and Studio semantics by Claude. First try a browser-side sampler through existing debug handles; if render/observer ordering cannot guarantee matching timestamps, a small explicit diagnostic callback is needed. No shipping-frame scanning, new runtime dependencies or model-in-the-loop behavior. Fixed-step scene seek can be reused for authored scenes; it cannot reconstruct the live AI/physics state. Exact gameplay replay would be separate work and is not included in the minimum.

An eventual bounded edit, only if diagnostics prove insufficient, could allow one named Shambler steering response/rate change against a hashed baseline, with speed, damage, reach, attack timing and collision behavior protected. Its configuration is currently frozen JavaScript, so safe candidate overrides/validation would be new work; this review does not propose enabling it now.

## Cost and how to decide

Planning estimates, not quotes: one developer familiar with this repository; use current local assets/browser tooling; no new dependencies. Diagnostics: **8–24 hours (1–3 days)** for one profile, shared scenario/sampling/manifest, focused views/metrics and failure checks. A sustained reliable shared browser/replay facility, multiple surfaces and extra rigs would increase that estimate. Expect **1–3 hours per materially changed rig/controller** to recalibrate; while animation work is active, budget roughly **1–3 hours/month** for scenario/browser/report upkeep, with overlap rather than simply adding both allowances.

A limited editing/reference pilot adds roughly **3–6 developer days** for one safe override/edit, validation/isolation, reference/profile checks and experiment handling. Broad Spider/Guardian/hand-path support would be further, unestimated work. Existing tools/brief discipline alone can be tried in an hour or two and may be enough; it has the lowest maintenance cost. No-build is reasonable if that captures the benefit or too little animation work remains. Diagnostics is selected because runtime evidence is both useful independently and the smallest presently identifiable gap.

For payback, count net human hours saved, failed attempts and ongoing upkeep. An 8–24-hour diagnostic investment saving a hypothetical 15 minutes per accepted task needs 32–96 tasks **before maintenance**; at one hypothetical hour saved, 8–24 tasks. Neither savings rate nor remaining task count is established. The already accepted Feral/Shambler/Leaper/Spider/Drowned/Military passes make it especially inappropriate to assume hundreds of remaining tasks. Ask how many new motion problems remain across unfinished types, Guardian, survivors and the marine before expanding.

Compare current workflow A (including existing videos/tests, not an artificially blind baseline) with B (same agent plus standardized final-state evidence) across three fresh comparable cases: turning/foot support, attack/recovery transition, and a style/readability adjustment. Keep model/budget/initial state/camera/requirements comparable and counterbalance variants. Define a correction as a user turn correcting a frozen requirement; log creative additions separately. Record per-case corrections, active human minutes, elapsed/model/tool cost, failures/abandonments, owner preference, defects and protected behavior. Three cases screen feasibility; repeat fresh variants before generalizing.

Use the proposal's 30% fewer median correction turns only as a **predeclared hurdle**, never a forecast. Require no increase in active human time, owner-rated equal/better quality, no protected-behavior regressions and no meaningful same-scene frame-time degradation with diagnostics disabled. Short ~60-fps historical captures do not establish performance of an unbuilt report. Stop if A already has zero/one correction, observation does not explain the errors, upkeep erases savings, or B's benefits are limited to the known example. Evaluate C only if B leaves repeated edit-precision failures.

## Cheapest discriminating test

Unresolved question: do better observations reduce corrections on an unchanged creative brief, or are most turns new artistic decisions?

**Do one no-code, 30-minute historical Feral palm-orientation diagnosis test.** Use `review/feral-claws-GP19850/before.html`, `before-feral-motion.js`, `before.png` and `before.webm` as read-only evidence; do not execute historical apply/prepare scripts or restore them over live files. Give a fresh-context agent a frozen request for downward, separated claws without changing ghost, speed, damage or attack timing. Compare a current-workflow attempt with one given a compact hierarchy/axis note and timestamped frames from the same saved footage, without revealing the final patch/handoff. Ask for diagnosis and a proposed minimal correction only. Count time and reviewer corrections, and check whether it identifies parent-chain compensation rather than guessing a local angle. If the evidence packet immediately improves that answer, trial diagnostics on fresh tasks; if it does not, do not build a workbench on this hypothesis. One historical success is a screening result, not proof of 30% savings.
